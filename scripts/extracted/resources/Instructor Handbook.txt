TITLE PAGE
EBOMI KINGDOM LEADERSHIP ACADEMY
Instructor Handbook
This Instructor Handbook is prepared for all trainers, mentors, practical instructors, departmental supervisors, facilitators and workplace preparation officers of EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy.
It explains the spiritual, academic, practical, moral, supervisory, mentoring and reporting responsibilities of every instructor.
Every instructor must understand that EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy is not only a training centre. It is a place where lives are formed, character is built, skills are developed, leadership is strengthened, discipline is established and young people are prepared to serve God, their families, their communities and the nation.
Instructors are not only teaching lessons. They are shaping destinies.

1. WELCOME TO INSTRUCTORS
Dear instructor,
You are welcome to EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy.
Your role is sacred, serious and highly important. You have been entrusted with the responsibility of teaching, guiding, correcting, supervising, mentoring and assessing young people who are being prepared for a life of purpose, service and productivity.
Some trainees may come from strong educational backgrounds. Others may come with limited formal education. Some may be unemployed graduates. Some may be secondary school leavers. Some may be orphans or vulnerable young people. Some may have suffered rejection, poverty, lack of direction, poor discipline or low confidence. Some may be talented, but untrained. Others may be wounded, but willing to learn.
Your task is not only to transfer knowledge. Your task is to help build people.
The Scripture says:
Proverbs 22:6“Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it.”
Training is more than talking. Training includes teaching, modelling, correcting, encouraging, supervising and holding people accountable.
At EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy, instructors must be firm but fatherly, disciplined but compassionate, professional but approachable, spiritual but practical, corrective but not humiliating.
You are expected to teach with excellence, live with integrity, correct with wisdom, supervise with diligence and serve as an example to the trainees.

2. VISION AND MISSION OF THE ACADEMY
2.1 Vision of the Academy
The vision of EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy is to raise a generation of God-fearing, disciplined, skilled, responsible and service-minded young leaders who will become agents of transformation in their families, communities, workplaces, ministries and nations.
The Academy is raising young people who will become light in society.
Matthew 5:14“Ye are the light of the world. A city that is set on an hill cannot be hid.”
2.2 Mission of the Academy
The mission of EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy is to provide spiritual formation, character training, leadership development, vocational skills, entrepreneurship education, community service experience and industrial exposure for young people who need direction, discipline, empowerment and practical preparation for life.
The Academy seeks to train the whole person:
1. Spirit.
2. Character.
3. Mind.
4. Hands.
5. Leadership.
6. Enterprise.
7. Service.
8. National responsibility.
Colossians 3:23“And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men.”
Every instructor must help trainees understand that work is not just for salary. Work is service before God.

4. THE INSTRUCTOR AS TEACHER
An instructor is first a teacher. Teaching means helping trainees understand what they did not understand before, practise what they could not do before and apply what they have learnt to real life.
A good instructor does not only speak. A good instructor explains, demonstrates, asks questions, listens, corrects and confirms understanding.
4.1 Duties of the Instructor as Teacher
The instructor must:
1. Prepare before every lesson.
2. Understand the topic clearly.
3. Teach in simple language.
4. Use examples that trainees can understand.
5. Connect lessons to real life.
6. Encourage questions.
7. Correct wrong understanding.
8. Give practical assignments.
9. Review previous lessons.
10. Confirm that trainees are learning.
2 Timothy 2:15“Study to shew thyself approved unto God, a workman that needeth not to be ashamed.”
An instructor must study before teaching. Unprepared teaching weakens the training process.
4.2 Teaching Principle
Teach clearly.Teach patiently.Teach practically.Teach repeatedly where necessary.Teach with love and authority.Teach for transformation, not just information.

5. THE INSTRUCTOR AS MENTOR
An instructor is also a mentor. Mentoring means guiding trainees beyond the classroom. Many trainees need encouragement, correction, direction and personal guidance.
Mentoring does not mean controlling trainees. It means helping them grow with wisdom and accountability.
5.1 Duties of the Instructor as Mentor
The instructor should:
1. Observe trainees carefully.
2. Identify trainees who are struggling.
3. Encourage trainees who are discouraged.
4. Correct trainees who are becoming careless.
5. Guide trainees in career and skill development.
6. Help trainees build confidence.
7. Help trainees develop discipline.
8. Listen with patience.
9. Pray for trainees.
10. Refer serious welfare or safeguarding issues to Academy leadership.
Proverbs 27:17“Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend.”
A mentor sharpens others. Instructors must help trainees become better.
5.2 Mentoring Boundaries
Mentoring must be done with wisdom, purity and accountability. Instructors must not use mentoring to manipulate, exploit, abuse or control trainees.

13. HOW TO PREPARE BEFORE EACH LESSON
Good teaching begins before the class starts.
13.1 Lesson Preparation Checklist
Before teaching, the instructor should:
1. Read the lesson at least once.
2. Identify the main point.
3. Identify the Scripture lesson.
4. Prepare simple explanations.
5. Prepare one real-life example.
6. Prepare one question for discussion.
7. Prepare one practical activity.
8. Prepare needed tools or materials.
9. Prepare assignment or assessment.
10. Prepare how to close the lesson.
13.2 Questions Every Instructor Should Ask
Before teaching, ask:
1. What should trainees know after this lesson?
2. What should trainees be able to do after this lesson?
3. What attitude should trainees develop?
4. What Scripture supports this lesson?
5. What practical example will make it clear?
6. What common mistake should I correct?
7. How will I know that trainees understood?
Proverbs 24:27“Prepare thy work without, and make it fit for thyself in the field; and afterwards build thine house.”
Preparation must come before delivery.

1. Opening prayer or brief reflection.

3. Review of previous lesson.
4. Introduction of today’s topic.
5. Reading of key Scripture.
6. Explanation of the topic.
7. Practical example or story.
8. Demonstration where needed.
9. Group activity or discussion.
10. Questions and answers.
11. Assignment or practical task.
12. Character application.
13. Summary.
14. Closing charge.
14.2 Time Discipline
Instructors must respect time. Do not spend the whole session talking without allowing practice. Do not waste training time with unrelated stories.
14.3 Participation
Encourage trainees to participate by:
1. Asking questions.
2. Inviting answers.
3. Allowing group work.
4. Giving practical tasks.
5. Asking trainees to explain in their own words.
6. Calling on quiet trainees gently.
7. Encouraging respectful discussion.
Nehemiah 8:8“So they read in the book in the law of God distinctly, and gave the sense, and caused them to understand the reading.”
Good teaching gives the sense and helps people understand.

18. HOW TO TEACH TRAINEES WITH DIFFERENT EDUCATIONAL BACKGROUNDS
The Academy will have trainees with different levels of education and confidence. Some may understand quickly. Others may need repetition.
Instructors must not shame trainees because they are slow to understand.
18.1 Teaching Principles for Mixed Backgrounds
1. Use simple language.
2. Explain difficult terms.
3. Write key words on the board.
4. Use local examples.
5. Demonstrate practical tasks.
6. Allow questions.
7. Repeat important points.
8. Use group work.
9. Pair stronger trainees with weaker trainees.
10. Encourage effort.
11. Avoid mockery.
12. Check understanding frequently.
18.2 How to Check Understanding
Ask trainees to:
1. Explain in their own words.
2. Demonstrate the task.
3. Give an example.
4. Write a short answer.
5. Work in pairs.
6. Complete a simple practical exercise.
7. Answer oral questions.
8. Teach back a small part of the lesson.
18.3 What Instructors Must Avoid
Instructors must avoid:
1. Insulting slow learners.
2. Comparing trainees in a humiliating way.
3. Using only complex English.
4. Assuming everyone understands.
5. Rushing through practical steps.
6. Ignoring quiet trainees.
7. Favouring only educated trainees.
8. Laughing at mistakes.
Proverbs 16:21“The wise in heart shall be called prudent: and the sweetness of the lips increaseth learning.”
Kind and wise speech increases learning.

19. HOW TO CONDUCT PRACTICAL DEMONSTRATIONS
Practical demonstrations are essential in every skill-based department.
19.1 Demonstration Steps
The instructor should:
1. State the purpose of the task.
2. Show the tools or materials.
3. Explain safety rules.
4. Demonstrate slowly.
5. Explain each step.
6. Repeat important steps.
7. Ask trainees what they observed.
8. Allow supervised practice.
9. Correct mistakes immediately.
10. Assess the finished work.
19.2 Demonstration Rule
Never assume that trainees know how to use tools, equipment, machines, animals, chemicals, digital devices, vehicles or office systems safely. Always explain first.
19.3 After Demonstration
After demonstration, trainees should:
1. Practise under supervision.
2. Ask questions.
3. Record what they learnt.
4. Clean the work area.
5. Return tools.
6. Reflect on mistakes and corrections.
Exodus 31:3-5“And I have filled him with the spirit of God, in wisdom, and in understanding, and in knowledge, and in all manner of workmanship,To devise cunning works, to work in gold, and in silver, and in brass,And in cutting of stones, to set them, and in carving of timber, to work in all manner of workmanship.”
God values skill, wisdom and workmanship.

24. CORRECTING TRAINEES WITHOUT HUMILIATION
Correction is necessary, but humiliation is harmful.
The goal of correction is restoration, discipline and growth, not destruction.
24.1 Principles of Godly Correction
1. Correct the behaviour, not the person’s worth.
2. Speak truthfully.
3. Avoid insults.
4. Avoid public shame where private correction is better.
5. Be specific about what is wrong.
6. Explain what must change.
7. Give the trainee opportunity to respond.
8. Apply consequences fairly.
9. Record serious or repeated issues.
10. Encourage improvement.
Galatians 6:1“Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness.”
Correction should aim at restoration.
24.2 Example of Good Correction
Poor correction:“You are useless and unserious.”
Better correction:“You came late again today. This is the third time this week. Punctuality is part of your training. You must report ten minutes early tomorrow and meet your mentor after class.”
24.3 Correction Must Be Fair
Instructors must not punish based on anger, personal dislike, tribal bias, gender bias, class bias or favouritism.

27. ASSESSING PRACTICAL SKILLS
Practical assessment checks whether trainees can perform tasks safely, correctly and responsibly.
27.1 Practical Skill Assessment Areas
Assess whether the trainee can:
1. Understand instructions.
2. Prepare tools and materials.
3. Follow safety rules.
4. Demonstrate correct procedure.
5. Use tools properly.
6. Complete the task.
7. Produce acceptable quality.
8. Keep the work area clean.
9. Return tools properly.
10. Explain what was done.
27.2 Practical Assessment Scale
Instructors may use the following simple scale:
1. Excellent: Performs safely, correctly and confidently.
2. Good: Performs well with minor correction.
3. Fair: Understands the task but needs more practice.
4. Weak: Struggles and needs close supervision.
5. Unsafe: Must not continue without further training.
27.3 Practical Feedback
Feedback should be specific.
Do not say only, “This is bad.”Say, “Your measurement was not accurate. Measure again from the correct point and mark clearly before cutting.”
Exodus 35:35“Them hath he filled with wisdom of heart, to work all manner of work.”
Skill requires wisdom, practice and correction.

Dear instructor,
You are not merely filling time on a timetable. You are forming lives.
The trainee in front of you may become a farmer, mechanic, accountant, administrator, builder, entrepreneur, public servant, project officer, hospitality professional, ICT worker, community leader, pastor, missionary, parent, employer or national reformer.
What you teach matters.How you teach matters.How you live matters.How you correct matters.How you supervise matters.How you treat trainees matters.

Be an example to the trainees. Let your life confirm your lesson.
