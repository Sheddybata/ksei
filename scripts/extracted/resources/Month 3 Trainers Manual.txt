EBOMI KINGDOM LEADERSHIP ACADEMY
365-Day Trainer’s Manual
Month 3
Monthly Theme: Biblical Leadership, Communication and Teamwork

MONTH 3 OVERVIEW
Purpose of Month 3
Month 3 focuses on leadership development, communication, teamwork and service. Trainees have already been introduced to the Academy’s vision in Month 1 and Kingdom character in Month 2. Month 3 now begins to shape them as servant leaders who can influence others positively.
This month teaches trainees that leadership is not pride, position, noise or control. True leadership is service, responsibility, sacrifice, example and influence.
Trainees will learn how to communicate respectfully, work in teams, solve problems, manage conflict, speak publicly, make decisions and serve communities.
Main Outcomes for Month 3
By the end of Month 3, trainees should be able to:
Explain biblical leadership.
Understand servant leadership.
Demonstrate humility in leadership.
Communicate clearly and respectfully.
Listen actively.
Work effectively in teams.
Resolve conflict peacefully.
Make wise decisions.
Take responsibility in group assignments.
Speak confidently in public.
Serve communities with compassion.
Lead by example.

WEEK 9
Weekly Focus: Biblical Leadership and Servant Leadership

DAY 57
Topic: Understanding Biblical Leadership
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Define biblical leadership.
Explain how biblical leadership differs from worldly leadership.
Identify examples of leaders in Scripture.
Understand leadership as service and responsibility.
Commit to becoming leaders who honour God.
2. Scripture
Matthew 20:26–28
3. Memory Verse
Mark 10:45
4. Teaching Notes
Biblical leadership is leadership that follows the values, principles and example of God. It is not based on pride, manipulation, selfish ambition or oppression. It is based on service, humility, responsibility, integrity and love.
Many people think leadership means being above others. In the Kingdom of God, leadership means serving others. Jesus taught that whoever wants to be great must become a servant.
Biblical leaders do not use people for personal gain. They protect, guide, build, correct, encourage and serve people.
Examples of biblical leaders include:
Joseph, who led with wisdom and integrity.
Moses, who led people out of bondage.
Joshua, who led with courage.
Deborah, who led with wisdom.
Nehemiah, who rebuilt with vision and compassion.
Daniel, who led with excellence and holiness.
Paul, who laboured to build the Church.
Jesus Christ, who is the perfect model of servant leadership.
A trainee must understand that leadership begins now, not after graduation. Every act of service, punctuality, honesty, humility and responsibility is part of leadership training.
5. Practical Activity
Each trainee should write the name of one biblical leader and list three leadership qualities seen in that person.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
What makes biblical leadership different from selfish leadership?
7. Case Study
A group leader was given responsibility over sanitation. Instead of helping the team, he only shouted orders and refused to work. Another leader joined his team, encouraged them and worked with them. His group completed the task faster and with joy.
8. Assessment
Assess through oral questions, biblical leader reflection and group participation.
9. Homework
Write one page on:
The kind of leader I want to become.
10. Leadership Application
Leadership begins with service. A person who refuses to serve is not ready to lead.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Servant Leadership
Trainees should practise leadership through service, not pride.

DAY 58
Topic: Jesus Christ as the Model Leader
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Identify leadership qualities in Jesus Christ.
Explain why Jesus is the perfect model of leadership.
Understand humility, compassion and sacrifice in leadership.
Apply the example of Jesus to daily conduct.
Commit to leading with Christlike character.
2. Scripture
John 13:12–15
3. Memory Verse
Philippians 2:5
4. Teaching Notes
Jesus Christ is the greatest example of leadership. He had power, yet He served. He had authority, yet He washed His disciples’ feet. He corrected people, but He also loved them. He spoke truth, but He did not lead with pride.
The leadership of Jesus shows:
Humility.
Compassion.
Courage.
Truthfulness.
Sacrifice.
Prayerfulness.
Obedience to God.
Service to people.
Patience with weakness.
Commitment to purpose.
Jesus did not lead for personal popularity. He led to fulfil the will of the Father and to save others.
Trainees must learn that Christian leadership is not about looking important. It is about becoming useful, faithful and responsible.
5. Practical Activity
The trainer should demonstrate servant leadership by performing a simple act of service, such as arranging chairs, helping clean a space or serving water, and then explain the lesson.
Trainees should then perform a simple act of service in groups.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
What leadership lessons can we learn from Jesus washing His disciples’ feet?
7. Case Study
A trainee was appointed group leader. Instead of feeling too important to work, he joined the group in cleaning and encouraged others. His humility made the team respect him more.
8. Assessment
Assess through participation in service activity, oral questions and group feedback.
9. Homework
Write five leadership qualities of Jesus that you want to practise.
10. Leadership Application
Christlike leaders do not lead by pride. They lead by example, humility and service.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Humility
Trainees should serve others without seeking recognition.

DAY 59
Topic: Leadership Begins with Self-Leadership
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Explain self-leadership.
Identify areas of personal life that must be managed.
Understand why a person must lead himself before leading others.
Practise personal discipline.
Commit to improving one area of self-leadership.
2. Scripture
Proverbs 16:32
3. Memory Verse
1 Timothy 4:16
4. Teaching Notes
Self-leadership means managing your own life before trying to manage others. A person who cannot control time, speech, appetite, emotions and habits is not ready to lead people.
Self-leadership includes:
Managing time.
Controlling emotions.
Speaking wisely.
Keeping promises.
Maintaining personal hygiene.
Completing assignments.
Praying consistently.
Reading and learning.
Avoiding wrong company.
Taking responsibility.
Many leadership failures begin with failure in private life. A person may appear strong in public but be weak in discipline.
The Academy trains trainees to lead themselves first. When a trainee wakes up early, attends devotion, completes assignments, respects rules and controls speech, he or she is practising self-leadership.
5. Practical Activity
Each trainee should complete a self-leadership checklist:
Time management.
Speech control.
Emotional control.
Assignment completion.
Prayer life.
Hygiene.
Respect for rules.
Response to correction.
Rate each area as:
Strong.
Improving.
Needs serious work.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why is it dangerous for a person to lead others when he cannot lead himself?
7. Case Study
A trainee wanted to become group leader, but he was always late, careless with assignments and easily angry. The trainer advised him to first learn self-leadership before seeking leadership over others.
8. Assessment
Assess through self-leadership checklist and oral questions.
9. Homework
Write one page on:
The area of my life I must lead better.
10. Leadership Application
Before leadership becomes public, it must first become personal.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Self-Government
Trainees should practise control over time, speech, emotions and habits.

DAY 60
Topic: Leadership as Responsibility, Not Position
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Explain leadership as responsibility.
Understand the difference between title and responsibility.
Identify irresponsible leadership behaviours.
Practise taking responsibility in small assignments.
Commit to faithful leadership in little things.
2. Scripture
Luke 12:48
3. Memory Verse
Luke 16:10
4. Teaching Notes
Leadership is not mainly about title. It is about responsibility. A person may have a title and still fail as a leader. Another person may have no title but influence others positively through responsibility.
Responsible leaders:
Keep promises.
Protect people.
Complete assignments.
Admit mistakes.
Solve problems.
Encourage others.
Report truthfully.
Avoid excuses.
Take initiative.
Serve faithfully.
Irresponsible leaders blame others, hide problems, seek praise, avoid difficult work and misuse authority.
In the Academy, every trainee will be tested with small responsibilities. How they handle small things will show whether they are ready for bigger opportunities.
5. Practical Activity
Assign each small group a responsibility, such as organising the classroom, preparing a short presentation, arranging tools or leading sanitation. Each group must report what they did and what they learnt.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why do some people want titles but avoid responsibility?
7. Case Study
A trainee was appointed to lead a group project. He enjoyed being called leader but did not organise the team, did not follow up and did not submit the report. The group failed because he wanted position without responsibility.
8. Assessment
Assess through group responsibility task, report and trainer observation.
9. Homework
Write five responsibilities you must take more seriously in the Academy.
10. Leadership Application
Leadership grows when responsibility is handled faithfully.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Responsibility
Trainees should take ownership of assigned duties.

DAY 61
Topic: Vision, Purpose and Direction in Leadership
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Explain vision in simple terms.
Understand why leaders need direction.
Identify the danger of aimless living.
Develop a simple personal leadership vision.
Connect purpose with service.
2. Scripture
Proverbs 29:18
3. Memory Verse
Habakkuk 2:2
4. Teaching Notes
Vision means seeing a preferred future and working towards it. A leader must know where he or she is going. Without vision, people become careless, distracted and confused.
Vision helps a person:
Stay focused.
Make better choices.
Avoid distractions.
Use time wisely.
Serve with purpose.
Inspire others.
Plan for the future.
A trainee with vision does not treat training casually. He or she understands that today’s discipline prepares tomorrow’s assignment.
Vision must be connected to service. A selfish vision only asks, “What can I get?” A Kingdom vision asks, “How can my life serve God and people?”
5. Practical Activity
Each trainee should write a simple personal leadership vision using this format:
The kind of person I want to become.
The people I want to serve.
The skills I need to develop.
The character I must build.
The problem I want to help solve.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
How does vision help a young person avoid distraction?
7. Case Study
Two trainees entered the Academy. One had no direction and followed every distraction. The other wrote clear goals and reviewed them weekly. After some months, the second trainee made better progress.
8. Assessment
Assess through personal vision statement and oral questions.
9. Homework
Refine your personal leadership vision and submit it for mentor review.
10. Leadership Application
Leaders give direction because they are guided by vision.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Purposefulness
Trainees should live and train with clear direction.

3. Memory Verse

Leadership requires sacrifice. A leader often gives time, energy, comfort and resources to serve others.

In the Academy, sacrifice may include:

Helping a weaker trainee.
Serving during sanitation.
Giving time to group work.
Listening patiently.
Accepting correction.

Putting community needs before personal convenience.

6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why is selfishness dangerous in leadership?

DAY 64
Topic: The Power of Communication
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Explain communication.
Understand why communication is important in leadership.
Identify good and bad communication habits.
Practise clear and respectful speech.
Commit to improving communication.
2. Scripture
Proverbs 18:21
3. Memory Verse
Colossians 4:6
4. Teaching Notes
Communication is the process of sending and receiving messages. It includes words, tone, listening, body language, writing and attitude.
Good communication helps people understand instructions, solve problems, build relationships and avoid confusion. Bad communication causes quarrels, mistakes, delays and offence.
In leadership, communication is essential. A leader must explain vision, give instructions, encourage people, correct mistakes and report progress.
Good communication should be:
Clear.
Respectful.
Truthful.
Timely.
Calm.
Complete.
Helpful.
Appropriate.
A trainee who learns to communicate well will be more useful in any workplace or community.
5. Practical Activity
Each trainee should introduce himself or herself in one minute, stating name, background, one skill they want to learn and one leadership quality they want to develop.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
How can poor communication affect group work?
7. Case Study
A group failed an assignment because the leader did not explain instructions clearly. Some members did the wrong task, and the report was incomplete.
8. Assessment
Assess through one-minute introduction, clarity and respectful speech.
9. Homework
Write one page on:
Why communication is important for leadership and work.
10. Leadership Application
Leaders must communicate clearly so people can follow direction.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Clarity and Respect
Trainees should speak clearly and respectfully.

DAY 65
Topic: Responsible Speech and Words That Build
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Explain responsible speech.
Identify harmful words.
Understand how words affect people.
Practise encouraging speech.
Commit to avoiding destructive speech.
2. Scripture
Ephesians 4:29
3. Memory Verse
Proverbs 15:1
4. Teaching Notes
Responsible speech means using words wisely. Words can build confidence, correct mistakes, bring peace and encourage growth. Words can also wound, divide, discourage and destroy.
Harmful speech includes:
Insults.
Gossip.
Lies.
Harsh criticism.
Mockery.
Complaining.
False accusation.
Public embarrassment.
Careless jokes.
Disrespectful tone.
A Kingdom leader should not speak anyhow. Speech must carry grace, truth and wisdom.
Correction should be firm but not insulting. Feedback should be truthful but not cruel.
5. Practical Activity
Pair trainees. Each person should practise giving constructive feedback on a simple task without insulting the other person.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
What types of speech destroy trust in a group?
7. Case Study
A trainee regularly mocked others during practical work. People became afraid to ask questions, and the group stopped learning freely.
8. Assessment
Assess through feedback practice, role-play and observation of speech.
9. Homework
Write five words or phrases you must stop using and five encouraging words you will practise.
10. Leadership Application
A leader’s words can either strengthen or weaken a team.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Wise Speech
Trainees should use words to build, not destroy.

DAY 66
Topic: Listening Skills and Understanding Others
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Explain active listening.
Understand why listening is important.
Identify poor listening habits.
Practise listening before responding.
Commit to becoming better listeners.
2. Scripture
James 1:19
3. Memory Verse
Proverbs 18:13
4. Teaching Notes
Listening is one of the most important communication skills. Many mistakes happen because people hear words but do not listen carefully.
Active listening means giving attention, understanding the message, asking questions and responding properly.
Poor listening habits include:
Interrupting.
Assuming.
Looking away.
Mocking.
Preparing an answer before understanding.
Ignoring instructions.
Talking too much.
Disrespecting the speaker.
Good listeners learn faster, avoid mistakes and build better relationships.
In technical training, poor listening can lead to accidents, wrong measurements, damaged equipment or poor service.
5. Practical Activity
The trainer should read a short instruction once. Trainees should write down what they heard and repeat the instruction accurately.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why do some people find it difficult to listen carefully?
7. Case Study
A trainee did not listen properly during a safety briefing. Later, he used a tool wrongly and almost caused injury.
8. Assessment
Assess through listening exercise and oral questions.
9. Homework
Practise active listening for one day and write what you learnt.
10. Leadership Application
Good leaders listen before they speak.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Attentiveness
Trainees should listen carefully before responding.

DAY 67
Topic: Asking Questions and Seeking Clarification
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Understand the value of asking questions.
Ask questions respectfully.
Know when to seek clarification.
Avoid pretending to understand.
Practise humble enquiry.
2. Scripture
Proverbs 20:5
3. Memory Verse
Proverbs 2:3–5
4. Teaching Notes
Asking questions is not a sign of foolishness. It is a sign of teachability. A trainee who does not understand should ask respectfully.
Many mistakes happen because people pretend to understand. In workplaces, pretending can cause errors, waste and accidents.
Good questions are:
Respectful.
Clear.
Relevant.
Timely.
Honest.
A trainee should not ask questions to embarrass others or argue unnecessarily. Questions should help understanding and growth.
5. Practical Activity
The trainer should explain a simple task. Trainees should then practise asking respectful clarification questions before doing the task.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why do some trainees pretend to understand when they do not?
7. Case Study
A trainee did not understand how to record inventory but was afraid to ask. He later made errors in the record. The supervisor told him that asking would have prevented the mistake.
8. Assessment
Assess through question practice and participation.
9. Homework
Write five respectful questions you can ask when you do not understand an instruction.
10. Leadership Application
Leaders seek understanding before making decisions.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Teachability
Trainees should ask questions with humility.

Preparation.
Clear voice.
Respectful tone.
Eye contact.
Simple language.
Clear message.

Confidence without pride.

1 Corinthians 12:18

1 Corinthians 14:40

Some people complain about problems but do nothing. Leaders do not only complain. They observe, think, pray, plan and act.
Simple problem-solving steps:
Identify the problem.
Understand the cause.
Consider possible solutions.
Choose a wise solution.
Act responsibly.
Review the result.
Trainees should learn to see problems as opportunities to serve.
5. Practical Activity
Groups should identify one problem within the Academy environment and propose a simple solution.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
What is the difference between complaining and problem-solving?
7. Case Study
A trainee noticed that tools were often misplaced. Instead of complaining, he suggested a labelled storage system. The workshop became more organised.
8. Assessment
Assess through group problem-solving presentation.
9. Homework

10. Leadership Application
Leaders take initiative to solve problems responsibly.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Initiative
Trainees should look for ways to help and improve situations.
