EBOMI KINGDOM LEADERSHIP ACADEMY
365-Day Trainer’s Manual
Month 6
Monthly Theme: Advanced Practical Training, Project Management and Workplace Readiness

MONTH 6 OVERVIEW
Purpose of Month 6
Month 6 focuses on strengthening trainees for real work. The previous months have built vision, character, leadership, communication, technical foundation, entrepreneurship and community service. Month 6 now moves trainees into deeper practical training, project management, workplace ethics, employability and readiness for higher responsibility.
The goal is to help trainees understand how real workplaces operate. They must learn how to receive instructions, complete tasks, manage time, work under supervision, handle tools and records, solve problems, submit reports, respect workplace rules and prepare for industrial attachment.
This month teaches that skill must become reliable performance. A trainee must not only know what to do; he or she must be able to do it with discipline, accuracy, safety, teamwork and accountability.
Main Outcomes for Month 6
By the end of Month 6, trainees should be able to:
Demonstrate improved practical competence.
Understand basic project management.
Plan and complete simple tasks.
Work under supervision.
Follow workplace rules and safety instructions.
Write simple work reports.
Understand workplace ethics.
Practise customer and supervisor communication.
Demonstrate employability behaviour.
Manage time and materials responsibly.
Participate in departmental project work.
Prepare for pre-industrial attachment training in Month 7.

DAY 141
Topic: Moving from Training to Performance
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Explain the difference between learning and performing.
Understand that practical training must produce visible competence.
Identify behaviours that improve practical performance.
Practise completing tasks according to instruction.
Commit to seriousness in advanced practical work.
2. Scripture
James 1:22
3. Memory Verse
Colossians 3:23
4. Teaching Notes
In the early months, trainees were introduced to vision, discipline, character, leadership and technical foundation. Month 6 requires trainees to move from hearing to doing.
Training is not complete until knowledge becomes performance. A trainee may understand a topic in class but still fail if he or she cannot apply it practically.
Performance means completing real tasks with:
Accuracy.
Safety.
Timeliness.
Good attitude.
Proper use of tools.
Respect for instructions.
Quality output.
Accountability.
Teamwork.
Continuous improvement.
A trainee must stop behaving like a visitor and start behaving like a responsible worker in training.
5. Practical Activity
Assign a supervised task in each department or group. The task should be simple but measurable. Examples include arranging tools, preparing a basic report, cleaning a work area, measuring a space, preparing materials or completing a department-specific task.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why is it not enough to know something if you cannot do it well?
7. Case Study
A trainee could explain safety rules in class but ignored them during practical work. The trainer reminded him that real learning is proven by behaviour, not words alone.
8. Assessment
Assess through practical task completion, attitude, accuracy and obedience to instructions.
9. Homework
Write one page on:
How I will move from learning to practical performance.
10. Leadership Application
Leaders must turn knowledge into action. People trust leaders who can deliver, not only talk.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Practical Seriousness
Trainees should treat practical work as preparation for real responsibility.

DAY 143
Topic: Receiving Instructions and Completing Tasks
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Understand the importance of instructions.
Practise listening carefully before starting work.
Ask respectful clarification questions.
Complete assigned tasks according to expectation.
Report completion properly.
2. Scripture
Proverbs 19:20
3. Memory Verse
James 1:19
4. Teaching Notes
Instructions are important because they guide work. A trainee who ignores instructions may waste time, damage materials, cause accidents or produce poor work.
Good task completion follows these steps:
Listen carefully.
Repeat the instruction if necessary.
Ask questions respectfully.
Identify tools or materials needed.
Start on time.
Follow safety rules.
Work carefully.
Report problems early.
Complete the task.
Submit or report completion.
Some trainees fail not because they are incapable but because they do not listen, they assume, or they start without understanding.
5. Practical Activity
The trainer should give a multi-step instruction once. Trainees must repeat the instruction, ask clarification questions and then complete the task in groups.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
What can go wrong when people begin work without understanding instructions?
7. Case Study
A trainee was asked to arrange materials by category. He did not listen carefully and mixed everything together. The work had to be repeated.
8. Assessment
Assess through instruction-following task, accuracy and completion.
9. Homework
Write one page on:
Why listening carefully is necessary for practical excellence.
10. Leadership Application
Leaders must understand instructions before giving direction to others.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Attentive Obedience
Trainees should listen carefully and follow instructions accurately.

DAY 144
Topic: Quality Control and Doing Work Properly
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Explain quality control.
Understand why quality matters.
Identify signs of poor-quality work.
Practise checking work before submission.
Commit to excellence.
2. Scripture
Ecclesiastes 9:10
3. Memory Verse
Proverbs 22:29
4. Teaching Notes
Quality control means checking work to ensure it meets the required standard. Poor-quality work wastes money, time and trust.
Quality matters in every department:
In construction, poor quality can endanger lives.
In catering, poor quality can cause sickness.
In farming, poor quality can reduce harvest.
In mechanics, poor quality can damage vehicles.
In finance, poor records can cause loss.
In ICT, careless work can create errors.
In administration, poor documentation causes confusion.
Trainees should develop the habit of checking their work before submitting it. Excellence is not only speed. It is doing the right thing properly.
5. Practical Activity
Give each group a simple task and provide a quality checklist. After completing the task, each group should inspect its own work before submitting.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why do some people submit poor work even when they can do better?
7. Case Study
A trainee painted a wall carelessly and left rough patches. When asked why, he said, “At least I finished.” The trainer explained that finishing badly is not excellence.
8. Assessment
Assess through practical task quality and use of checklist.
9. Homework
Write five ways to improve the quality of your work in your department.
10. Leadership Application
Leaders protect standards and do not celebrate careless work.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Excellence
Trainees should check their work and aim for quality.

Late start.
Poor planning.
Laziness.
Distraction.
Lack of materials.
Poor teamwork.
Unclear instructions.
Fear of asking questions.
Weak supervision.
Careless attitude.

DAY 146
Topic: Practical Work Reporting and Daily Logs
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Explain the purpose of daily work logs.

Report challenges honestly.
Identify lessons learnt from practical work.
Prepare for industrial attachment reporting.
2. Scripture
Habakkuk 2:2
3. Memory Verse
1 Corinthians 14:40
4. Teaching Notes
A daily work log is a simple record of what a trainee did, what was learnt and what challenges were encountered. It helps trainers track progress and helps trainees become accountable.
A simple work log should include:
Day number.
Department or activity.
Task assigned.
Tools or materials used.
What was completed.
What was learnt.
Challenge faced.
Correction received.
Safety issue observed.
Supervisor’s comment where applicable.
Work logs must be truthful. False reporting is dishonesty. Good reporting prepares trainees for industrial attachment.
5. Practical Activity
Trainees should write a daily work log based on the practical task completed during the day.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why is truthful reporting important in practical training?
7. Case Study
A trainee wrote that he completed a task when he had not done it. When the supervisor checked, the truth was discovered. His trust was damaged.
8. Assessment
Assess through completed daily work log, clarity and truthfulness.
9. Homework
Keep a simple daily work log for the next three training days.
10. Leadership Application
Leaders document work honestly so that progress can be measured.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Truthful Reporting
Trainees should record facts clearly and honestly.

DAY 148
Topic: Understanding Project Management
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Explain project management.
Identify examples of projects.
Understand why projects need planning.
Recognise the stages of a simple project.
Commit to organised work.
2. Scripture
Luke 14:28
3. Memory Verse
Proverbs 16:3
4. Teaching Notes
Project management means planning, organising and supervising work from beginning to completion. A project has a goal, tasks, people, time, materials and expected result.
Examples of projects include:
Renovating a classroom.
Planting a farm plot.
Repairing a road section.
Organising a training event.
Setting up a poultry unit.
Building a small structure.
Cleaning a community area.
Creating a business plan.
Preparing a catering service.
Setting up a record system.
Basic stages of a project:
Identify the need.
Define the goal.
Plan tasks.
Assign responsibilities.
Identify materials.
Set timeline.
Carry out work.
Monitor progress.
Complete the work.
Evaluate and report.
5. Practical Activity
Groups should choose one small project and write its goal, tasks, people needed, materials and timeline.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why do projects fail when there is no plan?
7. Case Study
A group wanted to clean and repair a classroom but had no plan. Some brought tools, others did not know their roles, and the work was delayed.
8. Assessment
Assess through group project outline and oral questions.
9. Homework
Write one small project you would like to carry out and list five steps needed.
10. Leadership Application
Leaders turn ideas into action through planning.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Organisation
Trainees should learn to plan before acting.

DAY 149
Topic: Setting Project Goals and Objectives
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Explain project goals.
Write simple project objectives.
Understand the difference between a goal and an activity.
Connect objectives to expected results.
Practise clear planning.
2. Scripture
Habakkuk 2:2
3. Memory Verse
Proverbs 29:18
4. Teaching Notes
A project goal is the main result a project wants to achieve. Objectives are specific steps or results that help achieve the goal.
Example:
Project goal: Improve cleanliness around the community health centre.
Objectives:
Clear waste around the building.
Unblock drainage.
Provide waste bins.
Educate users on cleanliness.
Submit a report after the work.
A goal should be clear. If the goal is unclear, people will not know what success means.
Trainees should avoid vague statements such as “We want to do something good.” They must state what exactly they want to achieve.
5. Practical Activity
Each group should write one project goal and three objectives for a practical or community service project.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why must a project have clear objectives?
7. Case Study
A group said they wanted to “help the school.” After discussion, they clarified the goal: “Repair ten broken classroom desks.” The clearer goal made planning easier.
8. Assessment
Assess through project goal and objective writing.
9. Homework
Write one project goal and five objectives for a project of your choice.
10. Leadership Application
Leaders provide clear direction so people know what to do.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Clarity
Trainees should communicate goals clearly.
