EBOMI KINGDOM LEADERSHIP ACADEMY
365-Day Trainer’s Manual
Month 4
Monthly Theme: Technical Foundation and Departmental Specialisation

MONTH 4 OVERVIEW
Purpose of Month 4
Month 4 introduces trainees more deeply to technical and vocational training. The first three months have focused on vision, Kingdom character, discipline, leadership, communication and teamwork. Month 4 now begins structured technical foundation and departmental specialisation.
The goal is to help trainees respect practical work, understand basic tools, follow safety rules, observe departmental procedures, begin simple hands-on practice and identify their area of interest or assignment.
Trainees must understand that practical skill is a gift, a responsibility and a pathway to service, employment, entrepreneurship and community development.
Main Outcomes for Month 4
By the end of Month 4, trainees should be able to:
Understand the dignity of technical and vocational work.
Identify major departments in the Academy.
Follow basic safety rules.
Handle tools and materials responsibly.
Understand workshop, farm, office and site discipline.
Participate in departmental rotation.
Begin basic practical assignments.
Show respect for craftsmen, technicians and professionals.
Demonstrate teamwork during practical sessions.
Connect skill training to employment, entrepreneurship and nation-building.

DAY 85
Topic: The Dignity of Labour and the Value of Practical Skills
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Explain the dignity of labour.
Understand why practical skills are important.
Reject pride towards vocational work.
Identify how skills create value.
Commit to learning with humility.
2. Scripture
Proverbs 14:23
3. Memory Verse
Ecclesiastes 9:10
4. Teaching Notes
The dignity of labour means that honest work has value before God and people. No honest work should be despised. Farming, building, welding, cooking, repairing, typing, accounting, cleaning, serving and managing resources can all glorify God when done with excellence.
Many people want white-collar jobs but do not respect practical skills. This mindset limits productivity. A nation grows when people can build, repair, produce, manage, organise and serve.
Practical skills help people:
Earn a living.
Solve problems.
Serve communities.
Create businesses.
Employ others.
Reduce dependence.
Build confidence.
Contribute to national development.
Trainees must enter technical training with humility. They must respect trainers, craftsmen, technicians, farmers, mechanics, builders, caterers, accountants and ICT workers.
5. Practical Activity
Each trainee should list five practical skills that are useful in society and explain one problem each skill can solve.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why do some people look down on practical work, and how can this mindset be corrected?
7. Case Study
A graduate refused to learn welding because he felt it was beneath him. Another young person learnt welding with humility and later opened a workshop that employed others.
8. Assessment
Assess through oral questions, group discussion and written skill list.
9. Homework
Write one page on:
Why honest work is honourable before God and useful to society.
10. Leadership Application
A good leader respects all honest workers and understands that every useful skill contributes to society.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Respect for Labour
Trainees should respect all departments and all honest forms of work.

4. Teaching Notes
Safety culture means making safety a habit in every place of work. Safety is not only for construction sites or workshops. It applies to farms, kitchens, offices, roads, ICT rooms, stores and public spaces.
Common hazards include:
Sharp objects.
Fire.
Electrical faults.
Slippery floors.
Poor tool handling.
Careless driving.
Unsafe lifting.
Chemical exposure.
Poor hygiene.
Disorganised workspaces.
Safety rules protect lives, tools, materials and reputation. A careless trainee can injure himself, injure others or damage equipment.
Trainees must learn to stop unsafe behaviour, report hazards and obey instructions.
5. Practical Activity
Take trainees around a classroom, workshop, farm, kitchen or office area. Ask them to identify possible hazards and suggest corrections.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why do people ignore safety rules, and what are the consequences?
7. Case Study
A trainee left water near an electrical socket. Another trainee noticed it and reported it before an accident happened. The trainer commended the trainee for safety awareness.
8. Assessment
Assess through hazard identification activity and oral questions.
9. Homework
List ten safety rules every trainee should obey in any department.
10. Leadership Application

1. Learning Objectives

2. Scripture
Luke 16:10
3. Memory Verse
1 Corinthians 4:2
4. Teaching Notes
Tools are important for practical work. A tool may look simple, but if it is lost, damaged or misused, work may stop.
Tool stewardship means using tools properly, cleaning them, storing them and reporting faults. Tools should never be stolen, hidden, wasted or used carelessly.
Basic tool discipline includes:
Know the name of the tool.
Know its purpose.
Use it only for the right task.
Carry it safely.
Clean it after use.
Return it to its place.
Report damage.
Do not borrow without permission.
Do not use tools for play.
Keep tool records where required.
5. Practical Activity
Display available tools from different departments. Trainees should identify each tool, state its use and demonstrate safe handling where appropriate.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
What happens when tools are not properly cared for?
7. Case Study
A group could not complete practical work because a trainee misplaced the measuring tape. The whole team learnt that one person’s carelessness can delay everyone.
8. Assessment
Assess through tool identification, safe handling and oral questions.
9. Homework
List ten tools you saw today and write one proper use for each.
10. Leadership Application
Leaders are faithful stewards of resources entrusted to them.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Stewardship
