EBOMI KINGDOM LEADERSHIP ACADEMY
365-Day Trainer’s Manual
Month 1
Monthly Theme: Orientation, Kingdom Identity, Discipline and Purpose

MONTH 1 OVERVIEW
Purpose of Month 1
Month 1 introduces trainees to the vision, values, discipline and spiritual foundation of EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy. It helps them understand that the Academy is not only a place for learning skills, but a place for transformation.
This month lays the foundation for the whole year. Trainees must learn the meaning of Kingdom character, discipline, responsibility, service, leadership, practical work and personal purpose.
Main Outcomes for Month 1
By the end of Month 1, trainees should be able to:
Understand the vision and mission of the Academy.
Accept the discipline and expectations of the programme.
Begin to develop daily spiritual habits.
Understand their Kingdom identity and personal purpose.
Practise punctuality, cleanliness, respect and responsibility.
Understand the importance of practical work.
Begin to work in teams.
Develop a personal growth plan.
Understand the connection between character and leadership.
Prepare for deeper training in Month 2.

WEEK 1
Weekly Focus: Understanding the Academy, Personal Commitment and Foundational Discipline

DAY 1
Topic: Understanding the Vision and Purpose of EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to explain the purpose of the Academy, identify the four core outcomes of the programme, and understand that the Academy is for transformation, not only employment training.
2. Scripture
Proverbs 22:29
3. Memory Verse
Colossians 3:23
4. Teaching Notes
EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy exists to raise dependable, disciplined, skilled, productive and God-fearing young men and women. It is not an ordinary training centre. It is a Kingdom-based academy for character, leadership, practical skills and nation-building.
The four core outcomes are:
Kingdom Character.
Professional Competence.
Leadership Capacity.
Entrepreneurial Ability.
A trainee who has skills but lacks character cannot be trusted. A trainee who has knowledge but lacks discipline will not become reliable. The Academy will train the spirit, character, mind, hands and heart of every trainee.
5. Practical Activity
Each trainee should write a personal commitment statement answering:
Why am I here?
What kind of person do I want to become?
What skills do I want to learn?
What habit must I overcome?
How do I want to serve God and society?
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:What kind of graduate should EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy produce?
7. Case Study
Daniel and Bala joined the same academy. Daniel was punctual, respectful and teachable. Bala was intelligent but careless and proud. After some months, Daniel was trusted with responsibility, while Bala was warned for poor attitude.
8. Assessment
Assess through oral questions, group presentation, personal commitment statements and observation of attitude.
9. Homework
Write one page on:The kind of person I want to become after one year at EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy.
10. Leadership Application
Leadership begins with personal discipline. A person who cannot manage himself cannot manage others.
11. Character Development Goal
Teachability and Discipline

DAY 2
Topic: Kingdom Identity: Knowing Who You Are in God
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to explain Kingdom identity, reject wrong labels and understand that their value comes from God, not from background, failure or present condition.
2. Scripture
1 Peter 2:9
3. Memory Verse
Jeremiah 29:11
4. Teaching Notes
Kingdom identity means seeing yourself the way God sees you. Many young people define themselves by poverty, family background, failure, unemployment or rejection. But God does not define people by their past. God defines people by His purpose.
Every trainee must know that he or she has value, purpose and potential. The Academy exists to help trainees discover, develop and use that potential.
Wrong identity produces wrong behaviour. Kingdom identity produces confidence, responsibility and purpose.
5. Practical Activity
Each trainee should write two lists:
Wrong labels I must rejectExamples: I am useless. I cannot succeed. Nobody believes in me.
Kingdom truths I must acceptExamples: I am created by God. I can learn. I can grow. I have a future.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:How can wrong identity affect a young person’s future?
7. Case Study
Aisha believed her background had destroyed her future. After encouragement and training, she began to pray, learn and practise. Over time, she became one of the most dependable trainees.
8. Assessment
Assess through oral questions, reflection exercise, group presentation and participation.
9. Homework
Write one page on:Three wrong beliefs I must reject and three Kingdom truths I must live by.
10. Leadership Application
A leader must know who he or she is. Insecure leaders may become proud, fearful or controlling.
11. Character Development Goal
Self-worth with Humility

DAY 3
Topic: Discipline as the Foundation of Greatness
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to define discipline, explain why discipline is necessary for success and identify personal areas that need discipline.
2. Scripture
Hebrews 12:11
3. Memory Verse
Proverbs 12:1
4. Teaching Notes
Discipline means training yourself to do what is right, whether it is easy or not. Discipline is not punishment. Discipline is preparation.
Every serious person needs discipline. Soldiers, doctors, teachers, builders, farmers, leaders and ministers all require discipline.
In the Academy, discipline includes waking up early, attending devotion, listening in class, participating in practical work, completing assignments, respecting authority and accepting correction.
Without discipline, talent is wasted.
5. Practical Activity
Each trainee should complete a discipline audit covering:
Waking up early.
Punctuality.
Cleanliness.
Listening.
Assignment completion.
Respect for authority.
Control of speech.
Prayer and Bible study.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:Why do many young people resist discipline?
7. Case Study
A carpentry apprentice refused correction about measuring wood properly. One day, he cut expensive wood wrongly and wasted materials. His master told him that correction is cheaper than waste.
8. Assessment
Assess through oral questions, discipline audit, punctuality and class behaviour.
9. Homework
Write ten personal discipline rules you will follow during the programme.
10. Leadership Application
A leader who lacks discipline will disappoint people.
11. Character Development Goal
Self-control

DAY 4
Topic: Punctuality, Time Management and Respect for Order
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to explain why time is valuable, understand punctuality as a sign of respect and prepare a simple daily time plan.
2. Scripture
Ephesians 5:15–16
3. Memory Verse
Psalm 90:12
4. Teaching Notes
Time is one of the most important resources God gives to every person. Money lost may be recovered, but time wasted cannot be returned.
Punctuality means arriving at the right place at the right time, prepared for the activity. Lateness communicates carelessness, disrespect and lack of seriousness.
In workplaces, punctuality affects productivity, trust and promotion. A trainee who respects time will become more trusted.
5. Practical Activity
Each trainee should prepare a daily time plan covering devotion, exercise, breakfast, lectures, technical training, practical work, mentoring, assignment time and evening reflection.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:What are the consequences of lateness in the workplace?
7. Case Study
A hotel worker was skilled but always late. One morning, an important guest needed service, but he was absent. The manager gave the responsibility to another worker who was always punctual.
8. Assessment
Assess through review of time plans, oral questions and punctuality observation.
9. Homework
Write one page on:How I will manage my time better during this programme.
10. Leadership Application
People lose confidence in leaders who are always late or disorganised.
11. Character Development Goal
Punctuality

2. Scripture
Romans 13:1
3. Memory Verse

Authority means recognised responsibility to guide, lead, correct and organise people. In the Academy, authority includes leaders, trainers, mentors, departmental heads and supervisors.
Rules are not created to oppress trainees. Rules maintain order, safety, fairness and discipline.
A trainee who cannot respect authority in the Academy may struggle in the workplace. Employers want people who can follow instructions and respect supervisors.
Respect means speaking properly, following due process and honouring others.

A trainee responds respectfully to correction.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:Why do some people find it difficult to accept correction?

Peter argued whenever he was corrected. He performed well in written tests but scored poorly in character and teamwork because people found it difficult to work with him.
8. Assessment
Assess through role-play, oral questions, respectful speech and conduct.
9. Homework

10. Leadership Application

11. Character Development Goal
Respect and Humility

DAY 6
Topic: Cleanliness, Environmental Sanitation and Stewardship
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to explain why cleanliness is part of discipline, practise environmental sanitation and understand stewardship.
2. Scripture
1 Corinthians 14:40
3. Memory Verse
Luke 16:10
4. Teaching Notes
Cleanliness reflects discipline, order and responsibility. A dirty environment can cause sickness, accidents, shame and poor productivity.
The Academy includes sanitation because trainees must learn that leadership begins with service. A person who is too proud to clean may not be ready to lead.
Stewardship means taking proper care of what has been entrusted to you, including buildings, tools, equipment, books and facilities.
5. Practical Activity
Divide trainees into teams and assign cleaning areas such as classroom, devotion area, workshop, farm area, toilets, dining area and office surroundings.
Each team should clean, arrange and report damaged items.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:What does a dirty environment say about leadership and discipline?
7. Case Study
In a workshop, sharp metal pieces were left on the floor. A trainee stepped on one and was injured. The accident happened because the workplace was not cleaned after practical work.
8. Assessment
Assess through inspection, teamwork observation, oral questions and cleanliness checklist.
9. Homework
Write one page on:Why cleanliness, order and stewardship are important for a Kingdom leader.
10. Leadership Application
Leaders take responsibility for the environment instead of waiting for others.
11. Character Development Goal
Responsibility and Stewardship

4. Teaching Notes

Skills help people solve problems, earn income, serve communities and become useful.
Every department matters, whether construction, farming, mechanics, hospitality, ICT, finance, administration or catering. No honest skill should be despised.
Trainees must approach practical work with humility and seriousness.
5. Practical Activity
The trainer should introduce available departments and allow trainees to observe tools, spaces and basic work areas.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:Why do some people look down on practical skills?
7. Case Study
A graduate refused to learn practical skills because he felt they were beneath him. Years later, he struggled, while another young person who learnt skills became productive.
8. Assessment
Assess through department observation notes.
9. Homework
Write three departments you are interested in and why.
10. Leadership Application
Leaders must respect work and workers.
11. Character Development Goal
Respect for Labour

10. Leadership Application
Faithfulness in small things prepares leaders for greater things.

Excellence
