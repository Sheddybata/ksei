EBOMI KINGDOM LEADERSHIP ACADEMY
365-Day Trainer’s Manual
Month 5
Monthly Theme: Entrepreneurship, Financial Stewardship and Community Service

MONTH 5 OVERVIEW
Purpose of Month 5
Month 5 focuses on helping trainees understand value creation, entrepreneurship, financial discipline, cooperative development and community service.
The Academy is not only training people to look for jobs. It is training people to solve problems, create value, manage resources, serve communities and become responsible contributors to society.
Trainees must understand that entrepreneurship is not only about making money. It is about identifying needs, solving problems, serving customers, managing resources faithfully and creating opportunities for others.
This month also teaches that leadership must serve communities. Trainees must learn to use their hands, skills, time and compassion to help others.
Main Outcomes for Month 5
By the end of Month 5, trainees should be able to:
Explain entrepreneurship in simple terms.
Understand value creation.
Identify business opportunities around them.
Prepare a simple business idea.
Understand personal financial management.
Practise saving and budgeting.
Keep simple records.
Understand costing and pricing.
Explain customer service.
Understand cooperative development.
Plan and participate in community service.
Connect skill training to employment, business and social impact.

WEEK 17
Weekly Focus: Entrepreneurship Mindset and Value Creation

DAY 113
Topic: Understanding Entrepreneurship
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Define entrepreneurship in simple terms.
Explain the difference between job-seeking and value creation.
Identify problems that can become business opportunities.
Understand entrepreneurship as service.
Commit to developing a productive mindset.
2. Scripture
Deuteronomy 8:18
3. Memory Verse
Proverbs 14:23
4. Teaching Notes
Entrepreneurship means identifying a need, solving a problem and creating value in a way that can generate income and serve people.
An entrepreneur is not only someone who owns a big company. A farmer, tailor, caterer, mechanic, welder, ICT worker, builder, trader, cleaner, consultant or service provider can be entrepreneurial if they solve problems and serve people well.
Many people wait for jobs, but entrepreneurs look for problems they can solve. A community problem may become an opportunity for service and income.
Examples:
Bad roads can create opportunity for road maintenance services.
Food demand can create opportunity for farming or catering.
Poor record keeping can create opportunity for bookkeeping services.
Broken equipment can create opportunity for repair services.
Lack of digital skills can create opportunity for ICT training.
Poor sanitation can create opportunity for cleaning services.
Scarcity of eggs or fish can create opportunity for poultry or fish farming.
Entrepreneurship must be ethical. A Kingdom entrepreneur should not cheat, exploit or deceive customers.
5. Practical Activity
Each trainee should write five problems in their community and suggest one possible business or service idea for each problem.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
What is the difference between waiting for opportunity and creating value?
7. Case Study
A young man complained that there were no jobs in his community. Another young person noticed that many farmers struggled to transport produce. He began organising produce transport with a small vehicle and later expanded the service.
8. Assessment
Assess through problem-and-solution list, oral questions and group participation.
9. Homework
Write one page on:
A problem in my community that can become a business or service opportunity.
10. Leadership Application
Entrepreneurial leaders do not only complain about problems. They think, plan and act responsibly.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Productive Thinking
Trainees should begin to see problems as opportunities for service and value creation.

2. Scripture
Matthew 5:16
3. Memory Verse
Proverbs 11:25
4. Teaching Notes
Value creation means doing something useful that helps people, solves problems, saves time, reduces stress, improves quality or meets a need.
People usually pay for value. They pay for food, transport, repair, building, cleaning, teaching, digital services, healthcare support, farm products, hospitality, clothing, tools, administration and other useful services.
A business idea must answer these questions:
What problem am I solving?
Who needs this solution?
How will I serve them?
What will make my service trustworthy?

What resources do I need?

Ask potential customers.
Observe demand.
Start small.
Try a sample product.
Compare prices.
Count basic costs.
Seek mentor advice.
Check competition.
Test quality.
Record feedback.
A wise beginning reduces waste. A foolish beginning may lead to debt, loss and discouragement.

9. Homework

10. Leadership Application
Leaders plan and test before committing resources.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Wisdom
Trainees should avoid rushing and learn to test ideas.

Luke 16:10–11
3. Memory Verse
1 Corinthians 4:2

Wrong attitudes to money include:
Greed.
Wastefulness.
Dishonesty.
Borrowing carelessly.
Showing off.
Gambling.
Spending without planning.
Refusing to save.
Misusing other people’s money.
Loving money more than God.

5. Practical Activity

6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why does poor money management create problems in families, businesses and communities?
7. Case Study

8. Assessment
Assess through oral questions, personal reflection and group participation.
9. Homework
Write one page on:

Income.
Giving or support where applicable.
Savings.
Food.
Transport.
Learning materials.
Personal care.
Emergency.
Debt repayment where applicable.
Balance.

2. Scripture
Habakkuk 2:2

Basic records include:
Income record.
Expense record.
Stock record.
Savings record.
Debt record.
Customer record.
Tools and equipment record.
Daily sales record.
Purchase record.
Profit or loss record.

1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:

2. Scripture
Luke 14:28
3. Memory Verse
Proverbs 24:3–4
4. Teaching Notes
Costing means calculating how much it takes to produce or provide a product or service. Pricing means deciding how much to charge customers.
Profit is what remains after all costs are removed from sales.
Costs may include:
Materials.
Labour.
Transport.
Electricity or fuel.
Packaging.
Rent where applicable.
Repairs.
Tools.
Time.
Waste.
