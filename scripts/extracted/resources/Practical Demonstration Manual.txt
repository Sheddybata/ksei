TITLE PAGE
EBOMI KINGDOM LEADERSHIP ACADEMY
Practical Demonstration Manual
This manual is prepared for trainers, practical instructors, artisans, craftsmen, technicians, farmers, administrators, ICT trainers, accountants, hospitality professionals, project officers and departmental supervisors of EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy.
It gives guidance on how to demonstrate practical skills safely, clearly and consistently across all departments.
The Academy trains young people for spiritual maturity, character, vocational competence, leadership, entrepreneurship, community service and industrial attachment. Therefore, practical training must not be careless. It must be safe, orderly, supervised, skill-based and character-forming.
Every trainer must understand that practical demonstration is not only about showing a task. It is about forming disciplined, careful, skilled, diligent and responsible trainees.

1. PURPOSE OF PRACTICAL DEMONSTRATION
The purpose of practical demonstration is to help trainees see, understand, practise and apply skills correctly.
Practical demonstration helps trainees to:
1. Understand a task before attempting it.
2. Learn correct procedures.
3. Observe safety rules.
4. Handle tools and materials properly.
5. Avoid common mistakes.
6. Practise under supervision.
7. Build confidence.
8. Develop discipline.
9. Connect theory with real-life work.
10. Prepare for industrial attachment and future employment or enterprise.
A trainee may forget what was only spoken, but will remember better when the trainer demonstrates clearly and allows supervised practice.
Ecclesiastes 9:10“Whatsoever thy hand findeth to do, do it with thy might.”
Practical training teaches trainees to work with their hands, minds and hearts.

2. PHILOSOPHY OF PRACTICAL TRAINING
The philosophy of practical training at EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy is that learning must produce transformation, competence and responsibility.
The Academy does not train trainees to become talkers only. It trains them to become doers.
Practical training must therefore be:
1. Biblical.
2. Safe.
3. Supervised.
4. Simple.
5. Step-by-step.
6. Hands-on.
7. Corrective.
8. Repetitive where necessary.
9. Character-forming.
10. Connected to real-life needs.
James 1:22“But be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only, deceiving your own selves.”
The Academy believes that trainees must learn by hearing, seeing, doing, reflecting and improving.

3. WHY TRAINEES MUST LEARN BY SEEING, DOING AND REFLECTING
3.1 Learning by Seeing
Trainees must first see the trainer perform the task correctly. Seeing helps them understand the process, tools, posture, order and safety requirements.
3.2 Learning by Doing
After seeing, trainees must practise. Skill grows through practice. A person cannot become competent only by watching.
3.3 Learning by Reflecting
After doing, trainees must reflect. Reflection helps them understand what they did well, what they did wrong, what correction they received and what they must improve.
3.4 The Training Cycle
The Academy’s practical training cycle is:
1. Explain.
2. Demonstrate.
3. Observe.
4. Practise.
5. Correct.
6. Repeat.
7. Record.
8. Reflect.
9. Assess.
10. Improve.
Proverbs 4:7“Wisdom is the principal thing; therefore get wisdom: and with all thy getting get understanding.”
Practical training must give both skill and understanding.

The trainer is responsible for safety, clarity, discipline and proper learning.
The trainer must:
1. Prepare the task before the session.
2. Prepare tools and materials.
3. Inspect the training area.
4. Explain the purpose of the task.
5. State safety precautions.
6. Demonstrate slowly.
7. Explain each step.
8. Repeat difficult steps.
9. Allow questions.
10. Supervise trainee practice.
11. Correct mistakes.
12. Stop unsafe behaviour immediately.
13. Assess performance.
14. Record evidence.
15. Give a character lesson.
2 Timothy 2:15“Study to shew thyself approved unto God, a workman that needeth not to be ashamed.”
A trainer must be prepared before demonstrating.

5. ROLE OF THE TRAINEE DURING DEMONSTRATION
The trainee must be attentive, respectful, disciplined and ready to practise.
The trainee must:
1. Arrive on time.
2. Dress appropriately.
3. Use required protective equipment.
4. Listen carefully.
5. Observe the trainer.
6. Ask questions respectfully.
7. Avoid playing with tools.
8. Wait for permission before practising.
9. Follow the steps shown.
10. Accept correction.
11. Keep the work area clean.
12. Record lessons in the logbook.
13. Add evidence to portfolio where required.
14. Report danger immediately.
15. Practise with humility and diligence.
Proverbs 1:5“A wise man will hear, and will increase learning; and a man of understanding shall attain unto wise counsels.”
A wise trainee listens before acting.

6. GENERAL DEMONSTRATION PROCEDURE
Every practical demonstration should follow this general procedure.
Step 1: Prepare Before the Session
The trainer prepares the tools, materials, safety equipment, workspace and demonstration steps.
Step 2: Gather the Trainees
Trainees should stand or sit where they can see clearly without crowding the trainer or creating danger.
Step 3: Introduce the Task
The trainer explains the title of the task, its purpose and why it matters.
Step 4: State Safety Rules
The trainer explains risks and safety precautions before touching tools or materials.
Step 5: Demonstrate Slowly
The trainer performs the task slowly, explaining each step.
Step 6: Repeat Key Steps
Difficult or risky steps should be repeated.
Step 7: Ask Questions
The trainer asks trainees what they observed and allows them to ask questions.
Step 8: Allow Supervised Practice
Trainees practise in pairs, groups or rotations depending on available tools and safety level.
Step 9: Correct Mistakes
The trainer corrects mistakes calmly, clearly and immediately.
Step 10: Assess and Record
The trainer assesses performance and records evidence in practical records, portfolios or logbooks.
Step 11: Reflect and Close
The trainer gives a character lesson, answers final questions and gives a closing charge.
1 Corinthians 14:40“Let all things be done decently and in order.”
Practical demonstration must be orderly.

7. SAFETY RULES BEFORE ANY DEMONSTRATION
Safety must come before speed, excitement or productivity.
7.1 General Safety Rules
1. Do not start any demonstration without trainer approval.
2. Inspect the training area before work begins.
3. Remove unnecessary objects from the work area.
4. Keep walkways clear.
5. Use proper tools for the task.
6. Do not use damaged tools.
7. Use protective equipment where required.
8. Do not allow horseplay.
9. Do not allow trainees to crowd dangerous areas.
10. Do not allow untrained trainees to use machines.
11. Keep first aid support available.
12. Keep fire safety equipment available where needed.
13. Stop work when serious danger is noticed.
14. Report every accident, injury or near miss.
15. Record safety incidents properly.
7.2 High-Risk Demonstration Warning
The following departments require extra caution:
1. Welding and Fabrication.
2. Construction Technology.
3. Road Construction.
4. Building Construction.
5. Heavy Equipment Operations.
6. Motor Vehicle Mechanics.
7. Agriculture and Agribusiness.
8. Poultry and Livestock.
9. Catering Services.
10. Hospitality Management where hot water, electricity, gas or chemicals are involved.
7.3 Trainer Safety Authority
The trainer has authority to stop any trainee who:
1. Refuses safety instructions.
2. Misuses tools.
3. Plays during practical work.
4. Distracts others.
5. Removes protective equipment.
6. Enters restricted areas.
7. Touches machines without permission.
8. Endangers self or others.
Proverbs 22:3“A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself: but the simple pass on, and are punished.”
Safety is part of wisdom.

9. HOW TO INTRODUCE A PRACTICAL TASK
Before demonstrating, the trainer should introduce the task clearly.
The introduction should answer:
1. What is the task?
2. Why is the task important?
3. Where is this task used in real life?
4. What tools and materials are required?
5. What safety risks are involved?
6. What result is expected?
7. How will performance be assessed?
8. What character lesson is connected to the task?
Example Introduction
“Today we will learn how to prepare a simple budget for a community clean-up project. This skill is useful in project management, public administration, office administration and entrepreneurship. We will list items needed, estimate costs and calculate the total. The safety issue today is not physical injury, but accuracy and honesty. A false budget can destroy trust.”

10. HOW TO DEMONSTRATE STEP BY STEP
A trainer should not rush practical demonstration.
Step-by-Step Method
1. Show the final expected result where possible.
2. Name each tool or material.
3. Explain the first step.
4. Perform the first step slowly.
5. Explain why the step matters.
6. Continue to the next step.
7. Pause after difficult steps.
8. Ask trainees what they observed.
9. Repeat key steps.
10. Summarise the full process.
Trainer Language
Use simple words such as:
1. “Watch carefully.”
2. “Do not touch yet.”
3. “This is the correct way.”
4. “This is the dangerous part.”
5. “This mistake can cause injury.”
6. “This step must not be skipped.”
7. “Now I will repeat it slowly.”
8. “Who can explain what I just did?”
11. HOW TO ALLOW TRAINEES TO PRACTISE
After demonstration, trainees should practise under supervision.
Practice Methods
1. Individual practice.
2. Pair practice.
3. Group practice.
4. Rotation practice.
5. Observation practice.
6. Assisted practice.
7. Peer demonstration.
8. Trainer-guided correction.
Rules Before Trainee Practice
Before trainees practise, the trainer must confirm:
1. The task has been demonstrated.
2. Safety rules are understood.
3. Tools are available and safe.
4. Trainees are properly dressed.
5. Group leaders are appointed where needed.
6. Trainees know what to do.
7. Trainees know what not to do.
8. Trainees know when to call the trainer.
9. The work area is safe.
10. The trainer can supervise properly.
Practice Rule
No trainee should practise a high-risk skill without direct supervision.

12. HOW TO CORRECT MISTAKES
Mistakes are part of learning, but dangerous mistakes must be stopped immediately.
12.1 Correcting Ordinary Mistakes
When a trainee makes a normal learning mistake:
1. Stop the trainee gently.
2. Explain the mistake.
3. Show the correct method.
4. Ask the trainee to repeat the step.
5. Encourage improvement.
6. Record repeated mistakes where necessary.
12.2 Correcting Dangerous Mistakes
When a trainee acts dangerously:
1. Stop the activity immediately.
2. Remove the tool or stop the machine if necessary.
3. Move the trainee away from danger.
4. Explain the danger clearly.
5. Decide whether the trainee may continue.
6. Record the safety concern.
7. Report serious cases to the supervisor or management.
12.3 Correction Without Humiliation
Do not insult trainees. Do not call them useless. Do not mock them. Correct the mistake, not their worth.
Galatians 6:1“Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness.”
Correction should restore and improve, not destroy.

15. HOW TO HANDLE PRACTICAL SESSIONS WITH LIMITED EQUIPMENT
In many Nigerian training environments, tools and equipment may be limited. This should not stop learning. Trainers must organise the class wisely.
15.1 Methods for Limited Equipment
1. Use trainer demonstration first.
2. Divide trainees into small groups.
3. Use rotation stations.
4. Assign observers with observation sheets.
5. Allow one group to practise while others record steps.
6. Use locally available substitute materials where safe.
7. Use diagrams before practical handling.
8. Use peer teaching after trainer approval.
9. Use mock practice for non-risky tasks.
10. Repeat the session over several days if needed.

5. Give safety rules.
6. Provide time limits.
7. Monitor each group.
8. Rotate roles regularly.
9. Assess both group and individual contribution.
10. Collect group reflections.

Do not allow one trainee to do all the work while others only watch. Every trainee must participate according to safety level and training stage.
Ecclesiastes 4:9“Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour.”

17. HOW TO PREVENT ACCIDENTS
Accidents are often caused by carelessness, rushing, poor supervision, poor tools, lack of protective equipment or disobedience.
17.1 Accident Prevention Rules
1. Plan the session properly.
2. Explain safety first.
3. Use correct tools.
4. Inspect tools before use.
5. Keep the work area clean.
6. Avoid overcrowding.
7. Use protective equipment.
8. Keep trainees focused.
9. Stop horseplay immediately.
10. Keep water away from electrical tools.
11. Keep fire sources controlled.
12. Handle sharp tools carefully.
13. Store chemicals safely.
14. Do not allow unapproved machine operation.
15. Report hazards immediately.
17.2 Near Miss Reporting
A near miss is an incident that could have caused injury but did not. Near misses should be recorded and used for learning.
Example: A trainee nearly touched a hot metal after welding. No burn occurred, but it must be reported and corrected.

18. HOW TO STOP UNSAFE BEHAVIOUR
Unsafe behaviour must be stopped immediately.
18.1 Steps to Stop Unsafe Behaviour
1. Use a clear command: “Stop now.”
2. Remove the danger if possible.
3. Move the trainee to a safe position.
4. Explain what was unsafe.
5. Ask the trainee to repeat the safety rule.
6. Decide whether the trainee can continue.
7. Record the incident.
8. Inform the departmental supervisor if serious.
9. Refer repeated unsafe behaviour for disciplinary review.
10. Do not allow pride or fear to delay action.
11. 2 Examples of Unsafe Behaviour
12. Welding without eye protection.
13. Grinding without face protection.
14. Working under a vehicle supported only by a jack.
15. Opening a hot radiator.
16. Running near construction materials.
17. Touching electrical cables with wet hands.
18. Using a knife carelessly in catering.
19. Handling sick animals without permission.
20. Standing near moving heavy equipment.
21. Ignoring trainer instructions.
Proverbs 27:12“A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself; but the simple pass on, and are punished.”
