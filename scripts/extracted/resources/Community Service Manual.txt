TITLE PAGE
EBOMI KINGDOM LEADERSHIP ACADEMY
Community Service Manual
This manual is prepared for trainees, trainers, mentors, community leaders, departmental supervisors, Academy management and all officers involved in community service at EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy.
Community service is a compulsory part of the Academy’s one-year training programme. It is designed to teach trainees humility, compassion, discipline, teamwork, leadership, responsibility, nation building and service before status.
The Academy does not only train young people to acquire skills. It trains them to use those skills to serve God, serve people, build communities and strengthen the nation.
Every trainee must understand that true leadership begins with service.

1. INTRODUCTION
EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy is raising a generation of disciplined, skilled and God-fearing young people who will not only seek personal success, but will also carry a burden for the transformation of society.
Many communities in Nigeria need support. Some need cleaner environments. Some need repairs in schools. Some need health awareness. Some need youth mentoring. Some need help for widows, orphans and elderly people. Some need support in farming, public property care, peacebuilding and community development.
Community service gives trainees the opportunity to move beyond classroom learning and practical workshops into real service among people.
Through community service, trainees learn that leadership is not pride. Leadership is not noise. Leadership is not title. Leadership is service.
Matthew 5:16“Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works.”
Trainees must shine through good works, humility, discipline and love.

2. PURPOSE OF COMMUNITY SERVICE
The purpose of community service is to train EBOMI trainees to serve people with humility, compassion, discipline and responsibility.
Community service helps trainees to:
1. Develop humility.
2. Develop compassion.
3. Learn teamwork.
4. Practise leadership.
5. Understand community needs.
6. Serve vulnerable people.
7. Build respect for community leaders.
8. Learn practical problem-solving.
9. Use vocational skills to help others.
10. Develop a nation-building mindset.
11. Learn public responsibility.
12. Appreciate the dignity of labour.
13. Represent Christ through good works.
14. Build bridges between the Academy and communities.
15. Prepare for responsible citizenship.
Galatians 5:13“By love serve one another.”
The Academy expects every trainee to serve with love, not pride.

3. BIBLICAL FOUNDATION FOR COMMUNITY SERVICE
3.1 Service
Mark 10:45“For even the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister.”
Jesus taught that true greatness is found in service. Trainees must learn to serve before they lead.

3.2 Love

3.3 Compassion

3.6 Responsibility

3.7 Nation Building

5. WHY SERVICE COMES BEFORE LEADERSHIP
A person who cannot serve should not be trusted to lead.
Leadership without service becomes pride.Leadership without humility becomes oppression.Leadership without compassion becomes selfishness.Leadership without responsibility becomes dangerous.
At EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy, trainees must first learn to carry chairs, clean environments, support the weak, listen to elders, work in teams, keep time, obey instructions and complete simple tasks before they are trusted with greater responsibilities.
Luke 22:26“But he that is greatest among you, let him be as the younger; and he that is chief, as he that doth serve.”
True leaders serve.

8. HOW TO SELECT A COMMUNITY SERVICE PROJECT
A community service project should be selected prayerfully, wisely and practically.
8.1 Selection Questions
Before selecting a project, ask:
1. Is this a real community need?
2. Will this project help people?
3. Is it safe for trainees?
4. Do we have permission?
5. Do we have enough supervision?
6. Do we have the tools and materials?
7. Can the project be completed within the available time?
8. Will the project protect the dignity of beneficiaries?
9. Will the project teach trainees useful lessons?
10. Can the impact be recorded?
8.2 Project Selection Criteria
A good community service project should be:
1. Needed.
2. Safe.
3. Approved.
4. Practical.
5. Affordable.
6. Supervised.
7. Beneficial.
8. Measurable.
9. Respectful.
10. Consistent with Academy values.
8.3 Projects to Avoid
Avoid projects that:
1. Are politically motivated.
2. Put trainees in danger.
3. Expose vulnerable people publicly.
4. Require skills trainees do not have.
5. Involve medical treatment without qualified professionals.
6. Involve heavy machinery without qualified operators.
7. Involve unsafe construction work.
8. Involve community conflict without proper leadership.
9. Are done only for publicity.
10. Lack permission from community leaders or relevant authorities.
9. HOW TO OBTAIN COMMUNITY PERMISSION
Community service must not be forced on a community. Permission must be obtained respectfully.
9.1 Who May Grant Permission
Permission may come from appropriate leaders such as:
1. Community head.
2. Ward head.
3. Village head.
4. Church or ministry leader.
5. School head teacher or principal.
6. Health facility head.
7. Market leader.
8. Local government representative.
9. Youth leader.
10. Women leader.
11. Property owner or authorised officer.
12. 2 Steps to Obtain Permission
13. Identify the community need.
14. Identify the right authority to meet.
15. Send an official request where necessary.
16. Explain the purpose of the Academy.
17. Explain the proposed service.
18. Explain expected dates and times.
19. Explain number of trainees involved.
20. Explain safety arrangements.
21. Ask for consent.
22. Record permission in writing.
23. Agree on community contact person.
24. Thank the community leader.
9.3 Permission Rule
No trainee team should enter a community or institution for official service without approval from Academy leadership and permission from the appropriate community or institutional authority.

11. HOW TO ORGANISE TRAINEES INTO TEAMS
Trainees should be organised properly before service begins.
11.1 Suggested Team Structure

2. Assistant team leader.
3. Safety officer.

5. Attendance officer.
6. Welfare officer.

12. Every trainee must belong to a team.

14. Every team must know its task.

16. Every team must know safety rules.
17. Every team must report to the trainer or supervisor.

Team size should depend on the activity. For safety and supervision, smaller teams are better for risky work.

1. Light cleaning: 5 to 10 trainees.
2. School support: 5 to 8 trainees.
3. Awareness campaign: 4 to 6 trainees.
4. Elderly or widow support: 2 to 5 trainees with mentor supervision.
5. Roadside support: 4 to 6 trainees with strong safety supervision.
6. Farm work: 5 to 10 trainees depending on tools and space.

13. SAFETY RULES DURING COMMUNITY SERVICE
Safety is compulsory. No service project is worth the loss of life or injury.
13.1 General Safety Rules
1. Attend the safety briefing.
2. Wear appropriate clothing.
3. Use gloves, boots or protective equipment where available.
4. Do not use tools without instruction.
5. Do not touch dangerous waste.
6. Do not handle medical waste.
7. Do not enter unsafe buildings.
8. Do not work near fast-moving traffic without proper control.
9. Do not carry loads beyond your strength.
10. Do not climb roofs, trees or unstable structures.
11. Do not handle electrical faults unless qualified and approved.
12. Do not work alone in isolated places.
13. Report injuries immediately.
14. Report threats or community conflict immediately.
15. Stop work if serious danger is noticed.
16. 2 Roadside Safety
17. Wear reflective clothing where available.
18. Stay away from moving vehicles.
19. Do not work in the middle of the road.
20. Use warning signs where available.
21. Work only under supervisor direction.
22. Watch motorcycles, tricycles, cars and trucks.
23. Do not bend into the road carelessly.
24. Do not argue with road users.
25. 3 Cleaning and Sanitation Safety
26. Do not touch sharp objects with bare hands.
27. Use rakes, shovels or sticks where appropriate.
28. Avoid broken bottles, rusty metals and needles.
29. Do not burn waste without approval.
30. Wash hands after work.
31. Keep drinking water separate from cleaning areas.
32. Avoid inhaling dust where possible.
33. Report hazardous waste.
34. 4 Farm Service Safety
35. Handle hoes and cutlasses carefully.
36. Keep distance when swinging tools.
37. Wear boots where possible.
38. Drink water during hot weather.
39. Watch for snakes, insects and thorns.
40. Do not handle chemicals without supervision.
41. Wash hands after farm work.
42. 5 Health Facility Support Safety
43. Do not touch blood, needles or medical waste.
44. Do not treat patients unless qualified and authorised.
45. Do not give medical advice unless qualified and authorised.
46. Wash hands regularly.
47. Respect patient privacy.
48. Follow health facility instructions.
49. 6 Vulnerable People Support Safety
50. Do not visit homes alone.
51. Do not enter private areas without permission.
52. Do not give food, medicine or money without approved plan.
53. Do not make promises.
54. Report safeguarding concerns immediately.
55. Protect the dignity and privacy of beneficiaries.
Proverbs 22:3“A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself: but the simple pass on, and are punished.”
Safety is wisdom in action.

15. HOW TRAINEES SHOULD RELATE TO COMMUNITY LEADERS
Community leaders must be treated with honour and respect.
15.1 Guidelines
1. Greet respectfully.
2. Introduce the Academy properly.
3. Listen carefully.
4. Do not interrupt rudely.
5. Speak through appointed team leaders or supervisors.
6. Do not make promises without authority.
7. Follow agreed instructions.
8. Thank leaders after service.
9. Report feedback to Academy management.
10. Maintain good relationships.
15.2 What Trainees Must Not Do
Trainees must not:
1. Argue with community leaders.
2. Disrespect local customs that do not violate Academy values.
3. Speak carelessly on behalf of the Academy.
4. Make political statements.
5. Take sides in community disputes.
6. Promise future projects without approval.
7. Criticise leaders publicly.
Romans 12:18“If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men.”

16. HOW TRAINEES SHOULD RELATE TO VULNERABLE PEOPLE
Vulnerable people may include orphans, widows, elderly people, people with disabilities, sick people, poor families, displaced persons and children.
16.1 Rules for Relating to Vulnerable People
1. Treat them with dignity.
2. Speak gently.
3. Do not laugh at their condition.
4. Do not expose their private lives.
5. Do not take pictures without consent and approval.
6. Do not make false promises.
7. Do not ask embarrassing questions.
8. Do not touch anyone inappropriately.
9. Do not enter private spaces without permission.
10. Report serious concerns to supervisors.
16.2 Compassion Without Pride
Helping vulnerable people does not make trainees superior. Trainees must serve with humility, knowing that every person has value before God.
Proverbs 14:31“He that oppresseth the poor reproacheth his Maker: but he that honoureth him hath mercy on the poor.”

17. PROTECTING THE DIGNITY OF BENEFICIARIES
Beneficiaries must never be treated like objects of publicity.
17.1 Dignity Protection Rules
1. Ask permission before taking photographs.
2. Avoid pictures that shame people.
3. Do not post beneficiary images online without approval.
4. Do not tell private stories without consent.
5. Do not exaggerate poverty for sympathy.
6. Do not force people to receive help publicly.
7. Do not compare beneficiaries negatively.
8. Do not use service to promote personal fame.
9. Do not expose children carelessly.
10. Do not reveal confidential information.
17.2 Language to Avoid
Avoid words such as:
1. “These people are useless.”
2. “They are helpless.”
3. “They are dirty.”
4. “We came to save them.”
5. “They have nothing.”
6. “They are backward.”
17.3 Better Language
Use respectful language such as:
1. “We are here to serve.”
2. “We are grateful for the opportunity to support.”
3. “We are working together with the community.”
4. “We came to learn and contribute.”
5. “Every person deserves dignity.”
18. AVOIDING PRIDE, POLITICS, MANIPULATION AND PUBLIC EMBARRASSMENT
Community service must remain pure and focused.
18.1 Avoiding Pride
Trainees must not serve to show off. Service must not become a display of superiority.
Philippians 2:3“Let nothing be done through strife or vainglory; but in lowliness of mind let each esteem other better than themselves.”
18.2 Avoiding Politics
Trainees must not use community service to support political campaigns, attack political groups or create political division.
18.3 Avoiding Manipulation
Trainees must not use gifts, service or help to control beneficiaries, pressure them or force them into decisions.
18.4 Avoiding Public Embarrassment
Trainees must not publicly embarrass communities or beneficiaries in speeches, pictures, reports or social media posts.
18.5 Media and Publicity Rule
Only authorised Academy officers may approve public pictures, videos, statements or social media posts about community service.

29. COMMUNITY SERVICE MISCONDUCT AND CORRECTION
Community service misconduct must be corrected because trainees represent the Academy.
29.1 Examples of Misconduct
1. Absence without permission.
2. Late arrival.
3. Refusal to work.
4. Disrespect to community leaders.
5. Insulting beneficiaries.
6. Fighting.
7. Stealing.
8. Damaging property.
9. Unsafe behaviour.
10. Taking unauthorised pictures.
11. Posting unauthorised content online.
12. Demanding money from community members.
13. Making false promises.
14. Political campaigning.
15. Sexual misconduct.
16. Leaving the service site without permission.
17. Mocking vulnerable people.
18. Falsifying service attendance.
19. Disobeying supervisors.
20. Bringing shame to the Academy.
29.2 Correction Process
Depending on the offence, correction may include:
1. Verbal correction.
2. Written warning.
3. Mentor referral.
4. Apology to affected person or community where appropriate.
5. Restitution for damage.
6. Community service repetition.
7. Suspension from certain service roles.
8. Disciplinary committee review.
9. Loss of community service clearance.
10. Management review for serious misconduct.
29.3 Correction Principle
Correction must be firm but fair. The goal is repentance, learning and restoration where possible.
Galatians 6:1“Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness.”

30. COMMUNITY SERVICE CLEARANCE FOR GRADUATION
Community service is required for graduation clearance.
30.1 Clearance Requirements
A trainee must:
1. Attend required community service activities.
2. Participate actively.
3. Obey safety rules.
4. Demonstrate good conduct.
5. Submit reflection forms.
6. Include evidence in portfolio.
7. Complete assigned service tasks.
8. Resolve any misconduct issues.
9. Receive satisfactory assessment.
10. Receive final clearance from the Academy.
30.2 Trainees Not Cleared
A trainee may not be cleared if he or she:
1. Repeatedly missed community service.
2. Refused assigned duties.
3. Displayed serious misconduct.
4. Falsified service records.
5. Failed to submit reflections.
6. Failed to include community service evidence in portfolio.
7. Brought shame to the Academy.
8. Failed to complete corrective action.
30.3 Final Clearance Decision
Final clearance should be based on attendance, conduct, participation, assessment, reflection, evidence and supervisor recommendation.

32. FINAL CHARGE TO TRAINEES
Dear trainee,
You were not brought to EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy only to receive. You were brought here to be transformed and to become useful.
A selfish person asks, “What can I get?”A servant asks, “How can I help?”A proud person seeks position.A humble person accepts responsibility.A weak leader wants to be seen.A true leader is willing to serve.
Community service will test your heart. It will show whether you can work without applause, clean without shame, help without pride, listen without argument and serve without demanding reward.
Do not look down on any community.Do not look down on poor people.Do not mock widows, orphans, children, elderly people or vulnerable families.Do not treat service as punishment.Do not serve only when cameras are present.Do not use service for pride, politics or personal fame.
Serve with love.Serve with humility.Serve with compassion.Serve with discipline.Serve with respect.Serve with diligence.Serve with the fear of God.
John 13:14-15“If I then, your Lord and Master, have washed your feet; ye also ought to wash one another's feet.For I have given you an example, that ye should do as I have done to you.”
Jesus served. Therefore, EBOMI trainees must serve.
Before you lead, serve.Before you command, obey.Before you rise, humble yourself.Before you seek honour, carry responsibility.Before you ask for a title, show faithfulness.
Final Scripture
Matthew 23:11“But he that is greatest among you shall be your servant.”
EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy is raising servant leaders, community builders and nation builders. Therefore, every trainee must learn to serve before they lead.
