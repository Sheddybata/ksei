TITLE PAGE
EBOMI KINGDOM LEADERSHIP ACADEMY
Portfolio and Logbook Manual
This manual is prepared for trainees, trainers, mentors, departmental supervisors and industrial attachment supervisors of EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy.
It explains how trainees should collect, record, organise and submit evidence of learning, character growth, practical skills, leadership, teamwork, communication, community service, entrepreneurship, employability and industrial attachment.
This manual also explains the difference between a portfolio and a logbook, how both should be completed, how they should be assessed and how they should be used for graduation readiness.
Every trainee must understand that training is not complete until there is evidence of learning, evidence of practice, evidence of growth and evidence of responsibility.

1. INTRODUCTION
EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy is not only interested in what trainees hear in class. The Academy is interested in what trainees become, what they can do, how they behave, how they serve, how they solve problems and how they represent Christ in practical life.
For this reason, every trainee must keep a portfolio and a logbook.
The portfolio shows evidence of progress and achievement.The logbook records daily and weekly learning activities.
Together, the portfolio and logbook help the Academy to see the trainee’s journey from admission to graduation.
They help trainers, mentors and supervisors answer important questions:
1. Has this trainee attended training faithfully?
2. Has this trainee completed assignments?
3. Has this trainee developed practical skills?
4. Has this trainee grown in character?
5. Has this trainee served the community?
6. Has this trainee developed leadership and teamwork?
7. Has this trainee prepared for employment or enterprise?
8. Has this trainee completed industrial attachment properly?
9. Is this trainee ready for graduation and certification?
Luke 16:10“He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much.”
A trainee who is faithful in recording small daily lessons is being prepared for greater responsibility.

2. MEANING OF A TRAINEE PORTFOLIO
A trainee portfolio is a well-organised collection of evidence that shows what a trainee has learnt, practised, completed, improved and achieved during the programme.
A portfolio is like a personal training file. It shows proof of learning and growth.
It may contain:
1. Personal information.
2. Completed assignments.
3. Practical task evidence.
4. Pictures of approved practical work.
5. Reflection notes.
6. Community service records.
7. Entrepreneurship plans.
8. Employability documents.
9. Mentor review pages.
10. Trainer review pages.
11. Industrial attachment evidence.
12. Final transformation reflection.
A portfolio answers the question:
What evidence shows that this trainee has learnt, grown and practised?

3. MEANING OF A TRAINEE LOGBOOK
A trainee logbook is a daily and weekly record of training activities, tasks, lessons learnt, practical work completed, challenges faced and supervisor comments.
A logbook is like a training diary. It should be completed regularly.
It may record:
1. Date.
2. Activity.
3. Topic learnt.
4. Practical task performed.
5. Tools or materials used.
6. Lessons learnt.
7. Challenges faced.
8. Character lesson learnt.
9. Questions for trainer or supervisor.
10. Trainer or supervisor signature.
A logbook answers the question:
What did this trainee do and learn each day or each week?

5. PURPOSE OF PORTFOLIO AND LOGBOOK
The portfolio and logbook are used to:
1. Track learning progress.
2. Record daily training activities.
3. Show evidence of practical skills.
4. Show evidence of academic work.
5. Show evidence of character growth.
6. Show evidence of leadership and teamwork.
7. Show evidence of community service.
8. Show evidence of entrepreneurship development.
9. Show evidence of employability preparation.
10. Show evidence of industrial attachment.
11. Help mentors guide trainees.
12. Help trainers assess performance.
13. Help supervisors verify work.
14. Help management make fair graduation decisions.
15. Teach trainees order, responsibility and accountability.
6. WHY EVIDENCE MATTERS
Evidence matters because words alone are not enough.
A trainee may say, “I learnt welding.”The portfolio should show pictures, task records, trainer comments and practical assessment.
A trainee may say, “I served the community.”The portfolio should show community service reflection, attendance records, supervisor comments and evidence of participation.
A trainee may say, “I improved in character.”The portfolio should show character reflections, mentor reviews, trainer comments and evidence of corrected behaviour.
A trainee may say, “I completed industrial attachment.”The logbook should show daily entries, supervisor signatures and final attachment assessment.
Evidence helps the Academy to be fair, honest and accountable.
2 Corinthians 13:5“Examine yourselves, whether ye be in the faith; prove your own selves.”
Evidence helps trainees examine themselves and prove their growth through clear records.

8. RESPONSIBILITIES OF TRAINEES
Every trainee is responsible for keeping his or her portfolio and logbook properly.
8.1 Trainee Responsibilities for Portfolio
The trainee must:
1. Collect evidence regularly.
2. Arrange documents in the correct sections.
3. Keep assignments neat.
4. Label evidence clearly.
5. Include dates where necessary.
6. Include trainer or supervisor comments where required.
7. Avoid false evidence.
8. Protect confidential information.
9. Submit portfolio for review when required.
10. Correct portfolio after feedback.
8.2 Trainee Responsibilities for Logbook
The trainee must:
1. Write daily entries honestly.
2. Record activities on the same day where possible.
3. Record lessons learnt.
4. Record practical tasks completed.
5. Record challenges faced.
6. Ask supervisor to sign where required.
7. Submit logbook for weekly review.
8. Keep logbook clean and safe.
9. Avoid copying another trainee’s logbook.
10. Avoid writing false activities.
8.3 Trainee Warning
A trainee who falsifies portfolio evidence or logbook entries may fail portfolio clearance, industrial attachment clearance or graduation clearance.
Proverbs 12:22“Lying lips are abomination to the LORD: but they that deal truly are his delight.”

9. RESPONSIBILITIES OF TRAINERS
Trainers are responsible for guiding, checking and assessing the learning evidence of trainees.
Trainers must:
1. Explain portfolio and logbook requirements.
2. Give clear assignments.
3. Sign practical task records where appropriate.
4. Review logbooks regularly.
5. Review portfolio sections monthly.
6. Give written feedback where necessary.
7. Correct poor record keeping.
8. Report false entries.
9. Assess academic and practical evidence fairly.
10. Recommend trainees for additional support where needed.
2 Timothy 2:15“Study to shew thyself approved unto God, a workman that needeth not to be ashamed.”
Trainers must help trainees become workers who are not ashamed.

10. RESPONSIBILITIES OF MENTORS
Mentors support character, spiritual growth, personal discipline and transformation evidence.
Mentors must:
1. Review character growth reflections.
2. Review spiritual growth evidence where appropriate.
3. Help trainees reflect honestly.
4. Encourage weak trainees.
5. Record mentoring comments.
6. Protect confidentiality.
7. Report serious welfare or safeguarding concerns.
8. Encourage repentance and improvement.
9. Confirm visible character growth where observed.
10. Support final transformation reflection.
Proverbs 27:17“Iron sharpeneth iron; so a man sharpeneth the countenance of his friend.”
Mentors help trainees become sharper, stronger and better.

11. RESPONSIBILITIES OF INDUSTRIAL ATTACHMENT SUPERVISORS
Industrial attachment supervisors confirm that trainees attended workplace training and participated responsibly.
Supervisors should:
1. Check trainee attendance.
2. Confirm daily tasks.
3. Sign logbook entries where appropriate.
4. Comment on punctuality.
5. Comment on practical skill.
6. Comment on workplace conduct.

Trainees must work faithfully during industrial attachment, whether or not Academy trainers are physically present.

1. HOW TRAINEES SHOULD COMPLETE THEIR LOGBOOK
The logbook should be completed daily or immediately after each major activity.
13.1 Logbook Completion Rules
Trainees must:
1. Write the date.
2. Write the activity or task.
3. Write what was learnt.
4. Write what was practised.
5. Write tools or materials used.
6. Write challenges faced.
7. Write character lesson learnt.
8. Write questions or support needed.
9. Get trainer or supervisor signature where required.
10. Keep the logbook neat.
13.2 What Makes a Good Logbook Entry
A good entry is:
1. Honest.
2. Clear.
3. Brief but meaningful.
4. Written regularly.
5. Connected to actual training.
6. Easy to understand.
7. Signed where necessary.
8. Free from exaggeration.
13.3 Poor Logbook Entry Example
“Today we did work. It was fine.”
13.4 Better Logbook Entry Example
“Today we learnt how to prepare a simple project budget. I listed materials, labour and transport costs for a classroom renovation project. I learnt that budgeting helps prevent waste. I need more practice with adding cost totals accurately.”

13. Gossip.
14. Unverified allegations.

Ephesians 4:25“Wherefore putting away lying, speak every man truth with his neighbour.”

30. CONFIDENTIALITY AND PRIVACY RULES
Trainees, trainers, mentors and supervisors must protect private information.
30.1 General Privacy Rules
1. Do not expose another trainee’s personal matter.
2. Do not include private counselling details in public portfolio sections.
3. Do not photograph people without permission.
4. Do not share workplace documents without approval.
5. Do not post portfolio evidence online without permission.
6. Do not include sensitive community information.
7. Do not expose vulnerable children, elderly people or sick people.
8. Do not share passwords or private digital files.
9. Do not use personal data carelessly.
10. Ask the trainer when unsure.
30.2 Confidential Mentor Notes
Mentor notes that contain private matters should be kept separately and only shared through approved Academy channels.
Proverbs 11:13“A talebearer revealeth secrets: but he that is of a faithful spirit concealeth the matter.”
A faithful trainee protects confidential matters.

38. FINAL CHARGE TO TRAINEES
Dear trainee,
Your portfolio and logbook are not ordinary documents. They are a record of your journey.
They show where you started.They show what you learnt.They show how you practised.They show how you changed.They show how you served.They show how you responded to correction.They show whether you were faithful with the opportunity given to you.
Do not treat your portfolio carelessly.Do not treat your logbook as a burden.Do not write lies.Do not copy another person’s work.Do not wait until the end before you begin.Do not hide your weaknesses.Do not ignore correction.
Write honestly.Collect evidence regularly.Organise your work neatly.Submit your records on time.Reflect deeply.Learn from mistakes.Let your growth be visible.
Ecclesiastes 9:10“Whatsoever thy hand findeth to do, do it with thy might.”
Do your portfolio with diligence.Complete your logbook with honesty.Serve with humility.Work with excellence.Grow in character.Prepare for responsibility.
Final Scripture
Psalm 90:17“And let the beauty of the LORD our God be upon us: and establish thou the work of our hands upon us; yea, the work of our hands establish thou it.”
May the Lord establish the work of your hands, and may your portfolio and logbook testify of faithful training, true growth and readiness for Kingdom service.
