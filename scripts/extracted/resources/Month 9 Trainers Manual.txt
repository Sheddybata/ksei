EBOMI KINGDOM LEADERSHIP ACADEMY
365-Day Trainer’s Manual
Month 9
Monthly Theme: Workplace Skill Development and Professional Discipline
MONTH 9 OVERVIEW
Purpose of Month 9
Month 9 continues the industrial attachment phase. Month 8 helped trainees settle into their host organisations, understand workplace culture, build relationships with supervisors and begin supervised learning.
Month 9 now focuses on deeper workplace skill development and professional discipline. Trainees should move beyond basic observation into more active participation under supervision. They must practise the skills of their departments, improve work quality, strengthen punctuality, build supervisor trust, complete daily logs, submit weekly reports and show discipline in real work situations.
This month teaches trainees that workplace growth requires consistency, humility, practice, safety, reporting, respect and patience.
Main Outcomes for Month 9
By the end of Month 9, trainees should be able to:
1. Participate more actively in supervised workplace tasks.
2. Identify and improve key workplace skills.
3. Follow workplace quality standards.
4. Practise professional discipline daily.
5. Strengthen punctuality and attendance.
6. Use tools, materials and records responsibly.
7. Communicate better with supervisors and colleagues.
8. Receive correction and apply feedback.
9. Keep accurate daily logs and weekly reports.
10. Demonstrate stronger teamwork.
11. Show improved confidence without pride.
12. Complete the second monthly attachment review.

DAY 225
Topic: Identifying the Main Skills Required in Your Placement
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
1. Identify the major skills needed in their attachment placement.
2. Understand the difference between general skills and department-specific skills.
3. Ask supervisors about important skills to develop.
4. Prepare a personal skill development list.
5. Commit to intentional learning.
2. Scripture
Proverbs 18:15
3. Memory Verse
Proverbs 1:5
4. Teaching Notes
Every workplace requires skills. Some skills are general, while others are specific to a department or profession.
General workplace skills include punctuality, communication, teamwork, record keeping, cleanliness, safety, reporting and respect.
Department-specific skills may include:
1. Measuring and marking in construction.
2. Tool handling in mechanics or welding.
3. Feeding and observation in livestock or fish farming.
4. Food hygiene in catering.
5. Customer service in hospitality.
6. Data entry in ICT or office administration.
7. Basic record keeping in finance.
8. Inventory control in procurement.
9. Report writing in administration.
10. Safety checking in workshops or sites.
11. Trainees must not assume they already know what matters. They should ask supervisors, observe experienced workers and write down the skills they need to develop.
5. Practical Activity
Each trainee should complete a skill identification sheet:
1. My placement department is:
2. Three general workplace skills I need are:
3. Five department-specific skills I need are:
4. One skill my supervisor says is important is:
5. One skill I want to practise this week is:
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why should trainees identify the skills needed in their placement early?
7. Case Study
A trainee in a farm placement thought his work was only to watch others. After speaking with the supervisor, he discovered he needed to learn feeding schedules, disease observation, record keeping and farm hygiene.
8. Assessment
Assess through skill identification sheet, supervisor comment and trainee reflection.
9. Homework
Write one page on:
The most important skill I need to develop during this attachment and why.
10. Leadership Application
Leaders identify what must be learnt before trying to perform independently.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Intentional Learning
Trainees should stop learning casually and begin learning deliberately.
DAY 226
Topic: Learning Through Repetition and Practice
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
1. Understand why repetition improves skill.
2. Practise assigned tasks patiently.
3. Avoid discouragement during slow progress.
4. Record repeated practice in their logbook.
5. Commit to steady improvement.
2. Scripture
Galatians 6:9
3. Memory Verse
Ecclesiastes 9:10
4. Teaching Notes
Skill grows through repeated practice. A trainee may not become good after doing something once. Repetition helps the hands, eyes, mind and judgement become stronger.
In workplaces, experienced workers often look skilful because they have practised many times. Trainees must be patient.
Repetition helps trainees improve in:
1. Accuracy.
2. Speed.
3. Confidence.
4. Safety.
5. Tool handling.
6. Record keeping.
7. Customer response.
8. Problem recognition.
9. Quality control.
10. Professional discipline.
11. Trainees must not say, “I already did it once.” Instead, they should ask, “How can I do it better next time?”
5. Practical Activity
Each trainee should identify one task they have repeated during attachment and write how their performance improved.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why do some trainees become discouraged when they do not master a skill quickly?
7. Case Study
A trainee struggled to record sales correctly. After repeating the same task under supervision for one week, he became faster and more accurate.
8. Assessment
Assess through logbook evidence, supervisor feedback and trainee reflection.
9. Homework
Write a short reflection on:
One task I must practise repeatedly until I improve.
10. Leadership Application
Leaders understand that mastery requires practice, patience and correction.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Patience
Trainees should practise without complaining or giving up.

Proverbs 13:20
3. Memory Verse

4. Teaching Notes
Experienced workers carry knowledge that may not be found in textbooks. They have learnt through years of practice, mistakes, correction and problem-solving.
Trainees should respect experienced workers, even if those workers have less formal education. A person can learn from craftsmen, farmers, mechanics, caterers, clerks, supervisors, drivers, administrators and office assistants.

2. What did you find difficult when you started?
3. What habit helped you improve?

5. Practical Activity
Each trainee should interview one experienced worker respectfully and record five lessons learnt.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why should trainees respect practical experience?
7. Case Study
A graduate trainee ignored an older technician because he felt more educated. Later, he realised that the technician understood the machines far better through years of practice.

Assess through interview notes and supervisor feedback.

Five things I learnt from an experienced worker in my placement.

1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:

3. Avoid demanding tasks beyond their level.
4. Practise under supervision.
5. Record supervised practice clearly.
2. Scripture
Matthew 7:7
3. Memory Verse
Proverbs 2:6
4. Teaching Notes
Trainees should not remain passive during attachment. They should ask for opportunities to practise, but they must do so with humility and respect.
A good request may sound like:
“Sir/ma, I have observed this task several times. If it is safe and convenient, may I assist or practise under your supervision?”
Trainees must never pressure supervisors or attempt tasks without permission. Some tasks require experience, safety clearance or special training.
Supervised practice helps trainees gain confidence while staying safe.

6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
What is the difference between asking for practice and acting without permission?
7. Case Study
A trainee in an ICT office asked politely to practise data entry after observing for several days. The supervisor allowed him to begin with simple entries under guidance.
8. Assessment
Assess through role-play, supervisor feedback and logbook entry.
9. Homework
Write one task you will ask to practise under supervision and how you will ask respectfully.
10. Leadership Application
