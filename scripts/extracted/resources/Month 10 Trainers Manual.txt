EBOMI KINGDOM LEADERSHIP ACADEMY
365-Day Trainer’s Manual
Month 10
Monthly Theme: Responsibility, Problem-Solving and Productivity
MONTH 10 OVERVIEW
Purpose of Month 10
Month 10 continues the industrial attachment phase with a stronger focus on responsibility, problem-solving and productivity. By this stage, trainees should no longer be only observing. They should now be more useful, more dependable and more confident under supervision.
Month 8 helped trainees settle into the workplace. Month 9 helped them develop workplace skills and professional discipline. Month 10 now trains them to take greater responsibility, solve problems wisely, improve productivity and contribute meaningfully to their host organisations.
Trainees must understand that productivity is not only being busy. Productivity means producing useful results with discipline, quality, honesty and good attitude.
Main Outcomes for Month 10
By the end of Month 10, trainees should be able to:
1. Take responsibility for assigned duties.
2. Complete tasks with fewer reminders.
3. Identify workplace problems early.
4. Suggest solutions respectfully.
5. Improve speed, accuracy and quality.
6. Use time, tools and materials wisely.
7. Communicate progress clearly.
8. Work better under pressure.
9. Contribute positively to team productivity.
10. Record evidence of workplace contribution.
11. Receive supervisor feedback on responsibility.
12. Prepare for leadership practice in Month 11.

DAY 253
Topic: Understanding Workplace Responsibility
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
1. Explain workplace responsibility.
2. Understand why responsibility builds trust.
3. Identify assigned duties in their placement.
4. Accept ownership of tasks.
5. Commit to faithful performance.
2. Scripture
Galatians 6:5
3. Memory Verse
Luke 16:10
4. Teaching Notes
Workplace responsibility means accepting the duties given to you and doing them faithfully. A responsible trainee does not wait to be pushed repeatedly. He or she listens, understands, acts, reports and improves.
Responsibility is shown through:
1. Punctuality.
2. Task completion.
3. Honest reporting.
4. Respect for supervisors.
5. Proper use of tools.
6. Care for materials.
7. Clean work habits.
8. Completion of daily logs.
9. Asking questions when unsure.
10. Reporting problems early.
11. A trainee may not be the most skilful yet, but responsibility makes the trainee trustworthy. Supervisors are more likely to teach and trust trainees who take duties seriously.
5. Practical Activity
Each trainee should list three duties regularly assigned in the workplace and explain how each duty should be handled responsibly.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why do supervisors trust trainees who take responsibility seriously?
7. Case Study
A trainee was asked to arrange tools after work. He did it every day without being reminded. After some time, the supervisor trusted him with more important duties because he had shown responsibility in small things.
8. Assessment
Assess through duty list, supervisor feedback and trainee reflection.
9. Homework
Write one page on:
How I will become more responsible in my attachment workplace.
10. Leadership Application
Leadership begins with responsibility. A person who avoids duty cannot be trusted with authority.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Responsibility
Trainees should take ownership of assigned duties without complaint.

DAY 261
Topic: Understanding Root Causes, Not Only Symptoms
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
1. Explain root cause.
2. Distinguish between symptoms and causes.
3. Ask wise questions about problems.
4. Avoid quick wrong conclusions.
5. Practise deeper thinking.
2. Scripture
Proverbs 20:5
3. Memory Verse
James 1:5
4. Teaching Notes
A symptom is what we see on the surface. A root cause is the deeper reason the problem is happening.
Example:
Symptom: Tools are always missing.Possible root cause: No tool register, poor storage, no assigned responsibility or weak supervision.
Symptom: Reports are always late.Possible root cause: Poor instructions, lack of template, weak time management or no follow-up.
To understand root causes, trainees should ask:
1. What exactly is happening?
2. When does it happen?
3. Who is affected?
4. What may be causing it?
5. Has it happened before?
6. What can prevent it from happening again?
7. Trainees should not accuse people quickly. They should observe and ask wisely.
5. Practical Activity
Choose one workplace problem and identify possible symptoms and root causes.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why is it dangerous to solve only the symptom and ignore the real cause?
7. Case Study
A farm kept losing young birds. At first, workers blamed the weather. Later, they found that poor hygiene was the root cause. Once hygiene improved, losses reduced.
8. Assessment
Assess through symptom-and-root-cause worksheet.
9. Homework
Write one problem and identify at least three possible root causes.
10. Leadership Application
Leaders think deeply before choosing solutions.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Wisdom
Trainees should avoid shallow judgement and seek understanding.
