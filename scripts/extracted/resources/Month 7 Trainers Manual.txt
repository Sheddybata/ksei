EBOMI KINGDOM LEADERSHIP ACADEMY
365-Day Trainer’s Manual
Month 7
Monthly Theme: Pre-Industrial Attachment Preparation and Final Classroom Evaluation

MONTH 7 OVERVIEW
Purpose of Month 7
Month 7 is the final month of the classroom and Academy-based training phase. It prepares trainees for industrial attachment, workplace placement, supervisor evaluation and real-life practical exposure.
The goal is to ensure that trainees do not enter industrial attachment carelessly. They must understand the meaning of attachment, workplace discipline, reporting, supervisor relationships, professional conduct, safety, confidentiality, daily logs, assessment expectations and the reputation of the Academy.
This month also includes final classroom evaluation. Trainees must be assessed in character, attendance, punctuality, leadership, teamwork, technical readiness, communication, practical competence and spiritual formation.
Main Outcomes for Month 7
By the end of Month 7, trainees should be able to:
Explain the purpose of industrial attachment.
Understand workplace expectations.
Demonstrate attachment readiness.
Prepare a personal attachment file.
Write daily and weekly reports.
Respect workplace supervisors.
Practise professional conduct.
Understand attachment safety and ethics.
Complete final classroom evaluations.
Present evidence of learning.
Receive feedback with humility.
Enter industrial attachment with discipline, integrity and readiness.

DAY 169
Topic: Meaning and Purpose of Industrial Attachment
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Explain industrial attachment.
Understand why attachment is part of the Academy programme.
Identify what trainees should learn during attachment.
Understand the difference between classroom training and workplace exposure.
Commit to taking attachment seriously.
2. Scripture
Proverbs 22:29
3. Memory Verse
Colossians 3:23
4. Teaching Notes
Industrial attachment is a period of supervised workplace learning. It allows trainees to apply what they have learnt in real organisations, businesses, farms, workshops, offices, construction sites, hospitality centres, ICT environments and public institutions.
Attachment is not a holiday. It is not a time to relax or become careless. It is a serious learning period.
During attachment, trainees should learn:
Workplace discipline.
Practical skills.
Professional communication.
Time management.
Safety culture.
Teamwork.
Customer service.
Reporting.
Problem-solving.
Respect for supervisors.
Real work expectations.
How organisations operate.
The classroom prepares trainees. Industrial attachment tests and strengthens them.
A trainee should enter attachment with humility. Even if he or she has learnt many things in the Academy, the workplace will require listening, observation and obedience.
5. Practical Activity
Each trainee should write answers to these questions:
What do I think industrial attachment means?
What do I hope to learn?
What workplace behaviour must I improve before attachment?
What skill do I want to practise?
How will I represent the Academy well?
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why should industrial attachment be treated as serious training, not free time?
7. Case Study
A trainee entered attachment thinking it was a holiday. He came late, avoided tasks and wrote poor reports. Another trainee arrived early, asked questions and learnt carefully. At the end, the second trainee received a strong supervisor recommendation.
8. Assessment
Assess through written reflection, oral questions and participation.
9. Homework
Write one page on:
What I must do to benefit fully from industrial attachment.
10. Leadership Application
Leaders prepare carefully before entering new assignments.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Seriousness
Trainees should approach attachment as a sacred opportunity for growth.

Respect supervisors.

DAY 180
Topic: Asking Questions and Learning by Observation
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Understand learning by observation.
Ask intelligent and respectful questions.
Avoid interrupting work carelessly.
Record lessons learnt.
Commit to active learning.
2. Scripture
Proverbs 18:15
3. Memory Verse
Proverbs 20:5
4. Teaching Notes
During attachment, trainees will not always be allowed to perform every task immediately. Sometimes they must observe first.
Observation is active learning. A trainee should watch carefully, listen, take notes and ask questions at the right time.
Good questions include:
What is the purpose of this task?
What safety rule should I remember?
What tool is used here?
What common mistake should I avoid?
How is quality checked?
How is this recorded?
May I practise under supervision?
Trainees should not ask questions rudely or interrupt dangerous work. They should wait for the proper moment.
5. Practical Activity
The trainer should demonstrate a simple task while trainees observe silently. After the demonstration, trainees should ask respectful questions and write lessons learnt.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why is observation important before practice?
7. Case Study
A trainee watched carefully for three days before being allowed to practise. Because he observed well, he performed better when given the opportunity.
8. Assessment
Assess through observation notes and quality of questions asked.
9. Homework
Write ten respectful questions you can ask during attachment.
10. Leadership Application
Leaders learn by observing before making decisions.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Attentive Learning
Trainees should observe carefully and ask wisely.

By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Understand character assessment.
Review leadership behaviour.
Receive mentor feedback.
Identify character strengths and weaknesses.
Commit to growth before attachment.
2. Scripture
1 Samuel 16:7
3. Memory Verse
Proverbs 20:11
4. Teaching Notes
Character assessment is essential because workplaces do not only test skill. They test honesty, respect, humility, punctuality, self-control, teamwork and reliability.
Character areas to assess include:
Integrity.
Truthfulness.
Respect.
Diligence.
Punctuality.
Teamwork.
Obedience.
Cleanliness.
Response to correction.
Self-control.
Accountability.
Service attitude.
Leadership potential.
Compassion.
Reliability.
The trainer should remind trainees that character growth continues. A weakness identified today can be corrected if the trainee is humble.
5. Practical Activity
Each trainee should complete a character self-assessment. Mentors should add brief comments where possible.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why is character more important than outward appearance alone?
7. Case Study
A trainee looked confident but was dishonest in small matters. Another was quiet but faithful, humble and reliable. The Academy trusted the second trainee more.
8. Assessment
Assess through self-assessment, mentor feedback and trainer observation.
9. Homework
Write one page on:
The character area I must strengthen before attachment.
10. Leadership Application
Leadership is sustained by character, not image.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Integrity and Reliability
Trainees should correct weak character habits before workplace placement.

DAY 188
Topic: Portfolio of Learning and Evidence Collection
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Understand a learning portfolio.
Gather evidence of training.
Organise documents and reflections.
Present progress clearly.
Prepare for final review.
2. Scripture
Psalm 90:17
3. Memory Verse
1 Corinthians 14:40
4. Teaching Notes
A portfolio is a collection of evidence showing what a trainee has learnt and done. It helps the Academy, mentors and future employers see growth.
A simple trainee portfolio may include:
Personal profile.
Commitment statement.
Character assessment forms.
Attendance record.
Practical work records.
Project reports.
Business idea draft.
Community service reflection.
Leadership reflection.
Certificates or commendations.
Mentor feedback.
Personal growth plan.
Attachment learning plan.
The portfolio should be neat and truthful. It should not contain false claims.
5. Practical Activity
Each trainee should organise a portfolio checklist and gather available evidence.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
How can a portfolio help trainees after graduation?
7. Case Study
A trainee kept good records of projects, reports and practical work. When applying for work, he could show evidence of training and responsibility.
8. Assessment
Assess through portfolio checklist and organisation.
9. Homework
Complete your learning portfolio for final review.
10. Leadership Application
Leaders document growth and preserve evidence of responsibility.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Evidence and Order
Trainees should organise proof of learning honestly.

DAY 191
Topic: Attachment Placement Briefing and Deployment Preparation
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Understand placement briefing.
Know what to do before reporting to placement.
Prepare required documents.
Understand communication channels.
Commit to orderly deployment.
2. Scripture
Proverbs 16:9
3. Memory Verse
Psalm 37:23
4. Teaching Notes
Placement briefing helps trainees understand where they are going, who they will report to and what is expected.
Before deployment, each trainee should confirm:
Host organisation name.
Department or unit.
Supervisor name where available.
Reporting time.
Required dress or PPE.
Documents needed.
Transport plan.
Academy contact person.
Reporting schedule.
Emergency contact process.
Trainees must not wait until the last moment to prepare. Confusion can be reduced through proper planning.
5. Practical Activity
Each trainee should complete an attachment deployment checklist.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
What problems can happen when trainees fail to prepare before reporting to placement?
7. Case Study
A trainee did not check transport and arrived very late on the first day. Another trainee planned ahead and arrived early with all documents.
8. Assessment
Assess through deployment checklist.
9. Homework
Prepare all documents and personal items needed for attachment.
10. Leadership Application
Leaders prepare logistics before assignment begins.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Prepared Order
Trainees should prepare early and avoid last-minute confusion.

DAY 192
Topic: Attachment Safety, Welfare and Emergency Response
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Understand attachment safety.
Identify welfare concerns.
Know how to respond to emergencies.
Report unsafe situations properly.
Commit to personal and workplace safety.
2. Scripture
Proverbs 22:3
3. Memory Verse
Psalm 121:7
4. Teaching Notes
Safety remains important during attachment. Trainees must not take unnecessary risks or ignore workplace hazards.
Trainees should know:
Workplace safety rules.
Emergency exits.
First aid contact.
Supervisor contact.
Academy contact.
PPE requirements.
Dangerous areas.
Reporting process for injury.
What to do if harassed or threatened.
What to do if asked to perform unsafe tasks.
Trainees must not prove courage by doing dangerous work without training. Safety is wisdom.
5. Practical Activity
Practise an emergency reporting role-play:
Injury at workplace.
Unsafe equipment.
Harassment concern.
Serious illness.
Transport emergency.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why should trainees not hide safety or welfare problems?
7. Case Study
A trainee was asked to operate equipment without training. He respectfully refused and reported to his supervisor and Academy mentor. His caution prevented danger.
8. Assessment
Assess through emergency response role-play.
9. Homework
Write five safety steps you will follow during attachment.
10. Leadership Application
Leaders protect life and report danger early.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Safety Wisdom
Trainees should avoid unnecessary risk and report danger.

Closing Charge for Month 7
Month 7 marks the end of the classroom training phase and the beginning of industrial attachment. Trainees must understand that they are now moving from controlled training into real-world learning.
The Academy has trained them in vision, character, discipline, leadership, communication, technical foundation, entrepreneurship, project work and workplace readiness. Now they must demonstrate these lessons outside the Academy environment.
Industrial attachment will test their punctuality, humility, honesty, skill, reporting, safety, teamwork, respect and spiritual consistency.
Every trainee must go out as a representative of Christ, the Academy, family and community.
Go with humility.Go with discipline.Go with courage.Go with integrity.Go with teachability.Go with excellence.Go as a servant leader.Go as a Kingdom ambassador.
Month 8 should begin the Industrial Attachment Phase, with the theme: Industrial Attachment Orientation and Workplace Integration.
