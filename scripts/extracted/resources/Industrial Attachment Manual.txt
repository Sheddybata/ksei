EBOMI KINGDOM LEADERSHIP ACADEMY
Industrial Attachment Manual
Five-Month Workplace Training Phase
1. Introduction
Industrial Attachment is the second major phase of the EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy one-year programme. After seven months of classroom instruction, character formation, technical training and supervised departmental practical work, trainees shall proceed to five months of workplace attachment.
Industrial Attachment gives trainees the opportunity to apply what they have learnt in real work environments. It helps them understand workplace discipline, professional conduct, teamwork, supervision, productivity, customer service, safety, reporting and accountability.
The Academy recognises that knowledge without practice is incomplete. It also recognises that skill without character is dangerous. Therefore, Industrial Attachment is not only about learning a trade or office skill. It is also about testing discipline, honesty, punctuality, humility, communication, integrity and readiness for real life.
The attachment period shall last for five months. During this period, trainees shall be placed in approved organisations, businesses, ministries, farms, workshops, offices, construction sites, hospitality centres, ICT centres, public institutions, NGOs or other suitable workplaces connected to their departments.

3. Purpose of Industrial Attachment
The purpose of Industrial Attachment is to expose trainees to real workplace experience and to prepare them for employment, entrepreneurship, ministry service, leadership and community impact.
The specific purposes are to:
Help trainees apply classroom knowledge in real work settings.
Develop practical workplace competence.
Build discipline, punctuality and respect for authority.
Strengthen character, integrity and moral responsibility.
Train trainees to work under supervision.
Expose trainees to workplace tools, systems, procedures and standards.
Improve communication, teamwork and problem-solving skills.
Help trainees understand customer service and public relations.
Build confidence for employment, apprenticeship or enterprise.
Teach trainees how to keep work records, logbooks and reports.
Expose trainees to the realities of the Nigerian work environment.
Help trainees identify business and career opportunities.
Strengthen the relationship between the Academy and industry partners.
Produce graduates who are spiritually grounded, technically useful and socially responsible.

6. Placement Organisation Approval Criteria
Before a trainee is placed, the Academy must ensure that the host organisation meets reasonable standards.
A suitable placement organisation should:
Be lawful and reputable.
Provide work relevant to the trainee’s department.
Have a supervisor or responsible officer.
Be safe enough for trainees.
Be willing to sign or acknowledge an attachment acceptance letter.
Be willing to evaluate the trainee monthly.
Be able to provide learning exposure, not merely use the trainee for menial labour.
Respect the Academy’s moral and disciplinary standards.
Be reasonably accessible to the trainee.
Avoid exposing trainees to unlawful, immoral or unsafe practices.
Not demand unofficial payments from trainees before acceptance.
Not require trainees to perform dangerous duties beyond their training level.
Respect the dignity and safety of female and male trainees.
Permit EBOMI mentors to visit or communicate with supervisors.
Allow the trainee to keep a logbook and submit reports.

Safety awareness.
Communication.
Respect for authority.
Departmental readiness.
Health and personal readiness.
Ability to represent the Academy properly.
Trainees with serious unresolved character or safety concerns may be delayed until corrected.

Each departmental trainer shall recommend suitable placement areas for each trainee based on:

Practical ability.
Character readiness.
Career interest.
Location feasibility.
Safety considerations.
Supervisor availability.

The Academy.

NGOs.

Address.
Contact person.
Nature of work.
Supervisor’s name.
Safety of the environment.
Relevance to trainee’s department.
Expected working hours.
Whether the organisation agrees to evaluate the trainee.

The host organisation shall provide acceptance through:

Written WhatsApp confirmation only where formally approved by the Academy.

Before trainees report to placement, the Academy shall organise an orientation covering:

Punctuality.
Dressing.
Safety.
Logbook use.
Weekly reports.
Confidentiality.
Communication with supervisors.
Mentor visitation.
Consequences of misconduct.
Step 8: Reporting to Host Organisation
On the first day, the trainee must:
Dress properly.
Arrive early.
Submit introduction letter where required.
Meet the workplace supervisor.
Learn workplace rules.
Record reporting details in logbook.
Inform EBOMI mentor that reporting has been completed.
Step 9: Monitoring and Mentoring
Throughout attachment:
Workplace supervisor shall monitor daily conduct.
EBOMI mentor shall follow up regularly.

Monthly evaluations shall be submitted.
Serious concerns shall be reported immediately.

At the end of attachment, each trainee must submit:

Monthly evaluation forms.
Mentor visitation forms.
Final attachment report.
Host organisation clearance or supervisor’s final comment.
Evidence of completed tasks where appropriate.
8. Duties of Trainees
During Industrial Attachment, trainees are ambassadors of EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy. Their behaviour will either strengthen or damage the reputation of the Academy.
Each trainee must:
Report to the approved placement organisation on time.
Obey workplace rules and lawful instructions.
Dress decently and appropriately.
Show respect to supervisors, staff, customers and visitors.
Keep daily logbook records.
Submit weekly reports to the Academy.
Participate in assigned tasks under supervision.
Ask questions respectfully when unsure.
Follow safety instructions.
Protect tools, equipment and materials.
Keep confidential information private.
Avoid gossip, arguments and rude speech.
Report workplace challenges to EBOMI mentor early.
Avoid lateness and absenteeism.
Inform both workplace supervisor and EBOMI mentor if sick or absent for genuine reasons.
Avoid leaving the workplace during working hours without permission.
Avoid misuse of phones during work.
Avoid collecting money, gifts or materials improperly.
Avoid signing documents without authority.
Avoid handling dangerous equipment without supervision.
Avoid representing the organisation publicly without approval.
Maintain spiritual and moral discipline.
Participate in mentor check-ins.
Submit final attachment report.
Represent Christ, EBOMI and the Academy honourably.
9. Duties of Workplace Supervisors
The workplace supervisor plays a major role in shaping the trainee’s attachment experience.
The workplace supervisor is expected to:
Receive the trainee properly.
Introduce the trainee to workplace rules.
Assign appropriate duties.
Provide reasonable supervision.
Ensure the trainee is not exposed to unsafe tasks beyond training level.
Teach workplace procedures where appropriate.
Monitor punctuality and attendance.
Monitor character and conduct.
Correct the trainee where necessary.
Report serious misconduct to the Academy.
Sign or review trainee’s logbook regularly.
Complete monthly evaluation forms.
Provide final assessment at the end of attachment.
Encourage good work habits.
Protect the dignity and safety of the trainee.
Avoid using trainees only for unrelated errands.
Avoid demanding unofficial payments from trainees.
Allow EBOMI mentors to visit or communicate regarding trainee progress.
Inform the Academy if placement is no longer suitable.
Recommend employment, apprenticeship or further training where appropriate.

A trainee must not go for attachment with pride. A trainee must go with a learning heart. The goal is not only to finish five months. The goal is to become employable, trustworthy, skilled, disciplined and useful.
