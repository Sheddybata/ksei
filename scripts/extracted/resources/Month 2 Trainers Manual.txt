EBOMI KINGDOM LEADERSHIP ACADEMY
365-Day Trainer’s Manual
Month 2
Monthly Theme: Kingdom Character, Integrity and Personal Development

MONTH 2 OVERVIEW
Purpose of Month 2
Month 2 focuses on the inner life of the trainee. The goal is to build strong Kingdom character, integrity, discipline, humility, diligence, emotional maturity and personal responsibility.
Month 1 introduced the vision and discipline of the Academy. Month 2 now begins deeper character formation. Trainees must understand that skill without character is dangerous. A person may know how to work, speak, build, repair, farm, cook, manage money or operate machines, but if that person lacks integrity, he or she cannot be trusted with responsibility.
This month teaches trainees that character is not optional. It is part of their training, assessment and graduation requirement.
Main Outcomes for Month 2
By the end of Month 2, trainees should be able to:
Understand the meaning of Kingdom character.
Practise honesty and truthfulness.
Demonstrate integrity in public and private.
Accept correction with humility.
Show responsibility in small and large assignments.
Practise self-control in speech, emotions and conduct.
Develop diligence and respect for work.
Improve personal hygiene and presentation.
Build emotional maturity and forgiveness.
Become more reliable, trustworthy and accountable.

WEEK 5
Weekly Focus: Integrity, Truthfulness and Accountability

DAY 29
Topic: Understanding Kingdom Character
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Explain the meaning of Kingdom character.
Identify character qualities expected in the Academy.
Understand why character is more important than talent alone.
Explain how character affects leadership, work and service.
Commit to personal character growth.
2. Scripture
Galatians 5:22–23
3. Memory Verse
Matthew 5:16
4. Teaching Notes
Kingdom character means living in a way that reflects the nature, values and standards of God. It is the evidence that a person is being shaped by God from the inside.
A trainee may be gifted, intelligent or skilful, but if he or she lacks character, that gift can harm others. Character determines whether a person can be trusted.
Kingdom character includes:
Love.
Joy.
Peace.
Patience.
Kindness.
Goodness.
Faithfulness.
Gentleness.
Self-control.
Honesty.
Humility.
Diligence.
Responsibility.
Respect.
Integrity.
The Academy must produce trainees who are not only employable, but trustworthy. Employers, communities, families and government institutions need people who can be trusted.
5. Practical Activity
Each trainee should write a personal character inventory.
They should list:
Three character strengths they already have.
Three character weaknesses they must improve.
One character quality they want God to develop in them this month.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why is character important in leadership and employment?
7. Case Study
A young man was very skilful in ICT, but he was dishonest with people’s data and money. After some time, people stopped giving him work, even though he was talented.
8. Assessment
Assess through oral questions, character inventory and group participation.
9. Homework
Write one page on:
Why character is more important than talent alone.
10. Leadership Application
Leadership depends on trust. People may admire talent, but they follow trustworthy character.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Integrity
Trainees should begin to examine their lives honestly and identify areas where they must grow.

DAY 30
Topic: Integrity in Public and Private Life
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Define integrity.
Explain why private behaviour matters.
Identify examples of integrity in daily life.
Understand that God sees what people may not see.
Commit to being the same person in public and private.
2. Scripture
Proverbs 10:9
3. Memory Verse
Luke 16:10
4. Teaching Notes
Integrity means being truthful, faithful and upright whether people are watching or not. It means your public life and private life agree.
Some people behave well only when supervisors are around. This is not integrity. Integrity means doing what is right because it is right, and because God sees the heart.
Integrity includes:
Not stealing.
Not lying.
Not cheating.
Not hiding mistakes.
Returning what is not yours.
Reporting truthfully.
Keeping promises.
Handling money faithfully.
Working properly without eye-service.
Taking responsibility when wrong.
A trainee who lacks integrity may pass examinations but fail in life. Integrity is one of the strongest foundations for leadership, business and ministry.
5. Practical Activity
The trainer should give practical examples and ask trainees to decide whether each action shows integrity.
Examples:
Returning extra money mistakenly given.
Signing attendance for an absent friend.
Pretending to work only when the supervisor appears.
Reporting a damaged tool honestly.
Hiding food or materials meant for the group.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why do some people behave differently when no one is watching?
7. Case Study
A trainee found money on the floor after class. Nobody saw him pick it up. He took it to the trainer. Later, the owner was found, and the trainee became trusted by others.
8. Assessment
Assess through oral questions, group discussion and response to practical examples.
9. Homework
Write five ways you will practise integrity in the Academy.
10. Leadership Application
A leader without integrity becomes dangerous because people trust leaders with power, money, information and responsibility.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Private Faithfulness
Trainees should practise doing what is right even when no one is watching.

DAY 31
Topic: Honesty and Truthfulness
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Explain the meaning of honesty.
Identify different forms of lying.
Understand the consequences of dishonesty.
Practise truthful reporting.
Commit to speaking the truth with wisdom and love.
2. Scripture
Proverbs 12:22
3. Memory Verse
Ephesians 4:25
4. Teaching Notes
Honesty means speaking and acting truthfully. It means refusing lies, deception, cheating and false appearance.
Dishonesty can appear in many forms:
Direct lies.
Exaggeration.
Hiding the truth.
False reports.
Cheating in assessment.
Forging signatures.
Pretending to be present when absent.
Taking what belongs to another person.
Blaming others falsely.
Making promises without intention to fulfil them.
Dishonesty destroys trust. Once trust is broken, it may take a long time to restore.
The Academy must train people whose words can be trusted. A trainee must learn to say the truth respectfully, admit mistakes and correct falsehood quickly.
5. Practical Activity
Each trainee should practise writing a truthful incident report.
Scenario:
A trainee accidentally damages a tool during practical work. Write a short honest report explaining what happened, when it happened and what should be done.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why do people lie even when the truth would help them grow?
7. Case Study
A trainee broke a chair and blamed another person. Later, the truth came out. The trainer was more concerned about the lie than the broken chair.
8. Assessment
Assess through incident report, oral questions and participation.
9. Homework
Write one page on:
How dishonesty destroys trust.
10. Leadership Application
A leader’s words must be trustworthy. If people cannot trust your words, they cannot trust your leadership.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Truthfulness
Trainees should speak the truth and avoid exaggeration, excuses and false reports.

DAY 32
Topic: A Clear Conscience Before God and People
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Explain the meaning of conscience.
Understand why conscience must be protected.
Identify actions that trouble the conscience.
Practise confession and correction.
Commit to living with a clean heart.
2. Scripture
Acts 24:16
3. Memory Verse
1 Timothy 1:19
4. Teaching Notes
Conscience is the inner witness that helps a person know when something is right or wrong. A healthy conscience warns a person before wrong action and troubles the person after wrongdoing.
People damage their conscience when they repeatedly ignore correction, justify sin or silence the truth.
A clear conscience is important for peace, worship, leadership and confidence. A person with a guilty conscience may become defensive, angry or fearful.
Trainees should learn to respond quickly when conscience warns them. They should confess wrongdoing, apologise, make correction and avoid repeating the mistake.
A clear conscience does not mean perfection. It means a person is honest before God and willing to correct wrong.
5. Practical Activity
Each trainee should write privately:
One wrong habit my conscience has warned me about.
One correction I need to make.
One step I will take this week.
The trainer should not force public sharing.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
What happens when a person keeps ignoring his or her conscience?
7. Case Study
A trainee cheated during a test. Nobody caught him, but he lost peace. Later, he confessed to the trainer and accepted correction. After that, he became more serious.
8. Assessment
Assess through oral questions, private reflection completion and respectful discussion.
9. Homework
Write one page on:
Why a clear conscience is important for a Kingdom leader.
10. Leadership Application
Leaders must live with a clear conscience because hidden wrong can destroy public trust.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Inner Honesty
Trainees should listen to conscience and respond to correction quickly.

DAY 33
Topic: Accountability and Openness to Correction
1. Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, trainees should be able to:
Define accountability.
Explain why correction is necessary for growth.
Identify wrong attitudes towards correction.
Practise receiving correction properly.
Understand the role of mentors and trainers.
2. Scripture
Proverbs 27:17
3. Memory Verse
Proverbs 15:31
4. Teaching Notes
Accountability means allowing trusted people to ask questions, give correction and help you stay on the right path. Nobody grows well without accountability.
Correction is not rejection. Correction is guidance. A wise trainee receives correction and improves.
Wrong responses to correction include:
Anger.
Arguing.
Blaming others.
Silent rebellion.
Mocking the trainer.
Refusing to change.
Gossiping after correction.
Right responses include:
Listening.
Thanking the person.
Asking how to improve.
Apologising where necessary.
Making correction.
Avoiding repetition.
5. Practical Activity
Role-play two scenes:
A trainee responds badly to correction.
A trainee responds maturely to correction.
The class should identify the difference.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
Why do people feel offended when corrected?
7. Case Study
A trainee was corrected for careless work. At first, he felt embarrassed. Later, he accepted the correction and improved. During assessment, he performed better than before.
8. Assessment
Assess through role-play, oral questions and observed attitude.
9. Homework
Write three corrections you have received in life and what you learnt from them.
10. Leadership Application
A leader who refuses correction will eventually fail publicly.
11. Character Development Goal
Character Focus: Teachability
Trainees should accept correction without pride or resentment.

2. Scripture
Proverbs 25:28
3. Memory Verse
2 Timothy 1:7
4. Teaching Notes
Self-control means the ability to govern your desires, emotions, words and actions. A person without self-control is easily controlled by anger, appetite, lust, laziness, fear, pride or pressure.
Self-control is needed in:
Speech.
Food.
Sleep.
Anger.
Money.
Sexual conduct.
Use of phone.
Friendship.
Time.
Work habits.
A trainee who lacks self-control may damage relationships, waste opportunities and fail assessments. Self-control is a fruit of the Spirit and a mark of maturity.

Teach the “Pause, Think, Pray, Respond” method.
When provoked, trainees should:
Pause.
Think.
Pray quietly.
Respond wisely.
6. Group Discussion
Discuss:
What areas of life require self-control among young people?
7. Case Study
A trainee became angry during group work and shouted at others. The group stopped working well with him. Later, he learnt to pause before speaking.
8. Assessment
Assess through oral questions and role-play.
9. Homework

Character Focus: Self-Control

James 1:14–15

1 Corinthians 10:13

Lying.

Laziness.
Anger.

Cheating.

James 4:6
