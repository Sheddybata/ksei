Departmental Training Module
Finance and Accounting

1. Department Overview
The Finance and Accounting Department trains young people to understand the basic principles, discipline, records, tools, ethics and practical skills required for handling money, keeping accounts, preparing budgets, recording transactions and reporting financial activities.
Finance deals with planning, managing and using money wisely. Accounting deals with recording, organising, checking and reporting financial information. Every organisation, ministry, business, farm, school, workshop, church, cooperative and household needs proper finance and accounting.
This department does not aim to turn trainees into chartered accountants within seven months. Rather, it gives trainees a strong foundation so they can keep simple records, prepare budgets, manage petty cash under supervision, issue receipts, record income and expenses, prepare simple reports, understand savings, support small businesses and prepare for industrial attachment.
Finance and Accounting is a department of trust. Anyone who handles money must be honest, accurate, orderly, discreet and accountable. A careless or dishonest finance worker can destroy a business, damage a ministry, cause debt, create conflict and lose public trust.
The department therefore trains trainees to see money as a stewardship before God. They must learn that financial records must be truthful, money must be handled faithfully, and every transaction must be properly documented.

2. Purpose of the Department
The purpose of the Finance and Accounting Department is to raise disciplined, honest and God-fearing trainees who can support businesses, ministries, organisations and community projects through sound financial record keeping and responsible money management.
The department aims to:
1. Teach trainees the meaning of finance and accounting.
2. Build basic understanding of income, expenses, assets, liabilities, capital and profit.
3. Train trainees in simple bookkeeping and record keeping.
4. Teach budgeting, savings, cost control and financial planning.
5. Train trainees in receipt writing, invoice awareness, petty cash records and cashbook preparation.
6. Introduce trainees to banking, reconciliation, payroll awareness and basic financial reporting.
7. Build honesty, accuracy, discipline, confidentiality and accountability.
8. Prepare trainees for industrial attachment in offices, businesses, cooperatives, farms, schools, churches, NGOs and accounting units.
9. Help trainees identify enterprise opportunities in bookkeeping and financial administration support.
10. Develop trainees who can serve communities through transparent, faithful and accountable financial service.

8. Safety Rules
Finance and Accounting may not involve physical danger like construction or welding, but it involves serious ethical, legal and reputational danger. Money and records must be handled carefully.
General Finance Safety and Integrity Rules
1. Do not handle real money without permission.
2. Do not keep organisational money personally.
3. Do not mix personal money with official money.
4. Do not borrow from cash entrusted to you.
5. Do not write false receipts.
6. Do not issue receipts without receiving money.
7. Do not receive money without issuing proper evidence.
8. Do not destroy financial documents.
9. Do not hide mistakes.
10. Do not share confidential financial information.
11. Do not alter records without approval.
12. Do not sign documents you are not authorised to sign.
13. Do not approve your own payment.
14. Do not create fake invoices.
15. Do not inflate prices dishonestly.
16. Do not pay money without proper approval.
17. Do not ignore missing cash or missing documents.
18. Do not disclose passwords.
19. Do not leave financial documents carelessly.
20. Obey trainer and supervisor instructions at all times.
Cash Handling Rules
1. Count cash carefully.
2. Count cash in a secure place.
3. Record cash immediately.
4. Issue receipt where required.
5. Keep cash in approved custody.
6. Report shortages immediately.
7. Report excess cash immediately.
8. Do not delay cash banking where banking is required.
9. Do not use cash for unauthorised expenses.
10. Submit cash reports on time.
Document Control Rules
1. Keep receipts safely.
2. File vouchers properly.
3. Number documents where required.
4. Do not tear out receipt pages carelessly.
5. Cancel wrong receipts properly under supervision.
6. Keep invoices attached to records.
7. Protect documents from water, fire and loss.
8. Keep digital records backed up.
9. Keep confidential files secure.
10. Submit records for review.
Digital Finance Safety Rules
1. Use strong passwords.
2. Do not share passwords.
3. Do not edit files without permission.
4. Save files with clear names.
5. Back up important work.
6. Do not delete records without approval.
7. Do not use public computers for confidential records without guidance.
8. Log out after use.
9. Protect spreadsheets from wrong entries.
10. Report digital errors immediately.
Safety Scripture
Proverbs 11:1“A false balance is abomination to the LORD: but a just weight is his delight.”
Safety Character Lesson
Financial safety is moral safety. A finance trainee must protect money, records, trust and the reputation of the Academy.

17. Departmental Closing Charge
Finance and Accounting is a sacred and practical field. Through proper finance, businesses grow, ministries remain accountable, schools run well, farms become profitable, projects are completed and communities develop.

The Academy is raising trainees who will not steal, falsify records or misuse entrusted resources. They must record truthfully, report honestly, budget wisely, protect documents, serve humbly and honour God with every figure.
