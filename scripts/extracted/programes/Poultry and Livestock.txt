Departmental Training Module
Poultry and Livestock
1. Department Overview
The Poultry and Livestock Department trains young people to understand the basic principles, safety rules, daily care practices, feeding systems, hygiene, disease prevention awareness, housing, record keeping and business opportunities in poultry and livestock production.
Poultry includes birds such as chickens, turkeys, ducks and guinea fowls. Livestock includes animals such as goats, sheep, pigs, cattle and rabbits. These animals provide meat, eggs, milk, manure, income, employment and food security for families and communities.
This department does not aim to turn trainees into veterinary doctors or advanced animal scientists within seven months. It gives trainees a strong foundation so they can care for animals responsibly, work under supervision, observe animal health, maintain clean housing, keep records, reduce losses, support farm operations and prepare for industrial attachment.
Poultry and livestock work requires patience, compassion, observation, hygiene, discipline, diligence and honesty. Animals cannot speak like humans, so trainees must learn to observe their behaviour, feeding, drinking, movement, droppings and general condition.
A careless trainee can cause disease spread, animal suffering, poor growth, death, financial loss and food safety problems. Therefore, the department must train trainees to care for animals with responsibility, kindness and wisdom.
2. Purpose of the Department
The purpose of the Poultry and Livestock Department is to raise disciplined, skilled and God-fearing trainees who can care for poultry and livestock responsibly, support animal production enterprises and contribute to food security and community development.
The department aims to:
1. Teach trainees the value of poultry and livestock production.
2. Introduce trainees to common poultry and livestock species.
3. Train trainees in animal housing, feeding, watering and hygiene.
4. Teach animal observation, disease prevention awareness and early reporting.
5. Train trainees in poultry brooding, egg production awareness, meat production awareness and basic livestock management.
6. Build discipline, compassion, patience, cleanliness and responsibility.
7. Prepare trainees for industrial attachment in poultry farms, livestock farms, veterinary support centres, feed stores, hatcheries, abattoirs, animal feed businesses and agribusiness organisations.
8. Help trainees identify honest enterprise opportunities in poultry and livestock.
9. Develop trainees who can serve communities through safe, productive and ethical animal care.

8. Safety Rules
Poultry and livestock work can expose trainees to scratches, bites, kicks, dust, manure, odour, infection, sharp tools, slippery floors and aggressive animals. Safety, hygiene and animal welfare must be repeated daily.
General Farm Safety Rules
1. Do not enter animal housing without permission.
2. Wear farm boots and protective clothing.
3. Wash hands before and after animal contact.
4. Do not eat inside animal houses.
5. Do not drink unsafe farm water.
6. Do not touch sick animals without supervision.
7. Do not handle aggressive animals without supervision.
8. Do not run inside animal areas.
9. Do not shout at or beat animals.
10. Keep tools away from animal movement paths.
11. Report bites, scratches and injuries immediately.
12. Report dead or sick animals immediately.
13. Keep floors dry where possible.
14. Clean spills and manure carefully.
15. Do not administer medicine without authorisation.
16. Do not vaccinate animals without authorised supervision.
17. Do not slaughter animals without approval and proper procedure.
18. Do not mix new animals with existing animals without instruction.
19. Keep visitors away from restricted areas.
20. Obey trainer and supervisor instructions at all times.
Poultry Safety Rules
1. Enter poultry houses only with permission.
2. Use footbath where provided.
3. Wash hands before and after handling birds.
4. Handle birds gently.
5. Do not overcrowd birds.
6. Do not frighten birds.
7. Remove dead birds only under supervision.
8. Keep drinkers clean.
9. Keep feed free from contamination.
10. Report unusual bird behaviour immediately.
Livestock Safety Rules
1. Approach animals calmly.
2. Do not stand directly behind large animals.
3. Do not tease animals.
4. Do not enter pens with aggressive animals without supervision.
5. Keep gates closed.
6. Use proper restraint only under supervision.
7. Keep children and visitors away from unsafe areas.
8. Report broken fences or weak pen doors.
9. Handle young animals carefully.
10. Wash hands after contact with manure or animals.
Biosecurity Rules
1. Control entry into animal houses.
2. Use footbath where available.
3. Wash hands regularly.
4. Clean tools after use.
5. Do not move tools between houses without cleaning.
6. Separate sick animals under supervision.
7. Report sudden deaths immediately.
8. Do not bring outside birds or animals into the farm without approval.
9. Keep feed storage clean and dry.
10. Dispose of waste safely.
Safety Scripture
Proverbs 22:3“A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself: but the simple pass on, and are punished.”
Safety Character Lesson
Safety is wisdom. A disciplined poultry and livestock trainee protects life, animals, farm resources and the reputation of the Academy.

Poultry and Livestock Business Ethics
Trainees must not:
1. Sell sick animals as healthy animals.
2. Sell bad eggs as good eggs.
3. Use false weights.
4. Lie about animal age, health or production.
5. Hide disease outbreaks.
6. Medicate animals without proper guidance.
7. Sell products during unsafe withdrawal periods.
8. Mistreat animals.
9. Steal feed or farm supplies.
10. Falsify production records.
11. Misuse cooperative funds.
12. Ignore biosecurity rules.
13. Overcrowd animals for quick profit.
14. Use customer money dishonestly.
15. Promise production results they cannot guarantee.

17. Departmental Closing Charge
Poultry and Livestock is a sacred and practical field. Through animal farming, families receive food, communities receive income, farms receive manure, businesses receive products and nations strengthen food security.

The Academy is raising trainees who will care for animals responsibly, build honest animal enterprises, protect public health, serve communities and honour God through faithful work.
