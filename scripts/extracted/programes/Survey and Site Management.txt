Departmental Training Module
Survey and Site Management
1. Department Overview
The Survey and Site Management Department trains young people to understand the basic principles of measurement, site layout, land observation, setting-out, levelling awareness, site organisation, site records, labour coordination, material control, safety and quality supervision.
Surveying helps construction work to begin in the right position, at the right level, with the right measurements. Site management helps construction work to continue in an orderly, safe, disciplined and productive manner.
A poorly measured site can lead to wrong building positions, poor drainage, road errors, boundary conflicts, wasted materials and unsafe structures. A poorly managed site can lead to accidents, theft, delays, confusion, poor quality and waste.
This department does not aim to turn trainees into fully qualified surveyors, engineers or site managers within seven months. Rather, it gives them a strong foundation so they can assist professionals, understand site operations, keep simple records, support site discipline and prepare for industrial attachment.
Survey and Site Management requires accuracy, honesty, order, patience, clear communication and respect for authority. A trainee in this department must learn to measure carefully, record truthfully, organise responsibly and serve the whole project with discipline.
2. Purpose of the Department
The purpose of the Survey and Site Management Department is to raise disciplined, accurate, safety-conscious and God-fearing trainees who can support construction, road, building and infrastructure projects under supervision.
The department aims to:
1. Teach trainees the importance of accurate measurement.
2. Introduce trainees to basic surveying tools and site management practices.
3. Train trainees in simple measurement, marking, setting-out and levelling awareness.
4. Teach site organisation, tool control, material records and daily reporting.
5. Build honesty, accuracy, punctuality, teamwork and responsibility.
6. Prepare trainees for industrial attachment on building sites, road sites, survey teams, project offices and public works departments.
7. Help trainees understand enterprise opportunities in site support services.
8. Develop trainees who can serve construction projects through order, safety and accountability.

5. Weekly Topics for Seven Months
The seven-month classroom and practical phase is divided into twenty-eight training weeks. Each week should include teaching, demonstration, supervised practical work, safety instruction, group discussion, record keeping and character formation.

Week 1: Introduction to Survey and Site Management
Teaching Focus
Meaning of surveying, meaning of site management, importance of measurement, site order and the role of survey and site support workers.
Scripture for the Week
Proverbs 24:3“Through wisdom is an house builded; and by understanding it is established.”
Daily Practical Activities
1. Day 1: Walk around the Academy environment and identify buildings, roads, drains, open spaces and site features.
2. Day 2: Discuss why construction projects need measurement and order.
3. Day 3: Identify people involved in site work, such as surveyors, engineers, site supervisors, foremen, masons, carpenters and labourers.
4. Day 4: Discuss what can go wrong when a site is poorly measured or poorly managed.

Character Lesson
A wise worker does not guess. Survey and site management teach trainees to think, measure, plan and act with understanding.

8. Safety Rules
Survey and site management work can be dangerous because trainees may work around trenches, buildings, roads, moving vehicles, equipment, materials and uneven ground.
General Site Safety Rules
1. Do not enter any site without permission.
2. Wear required protective equipment.
3. Do not run on site.
4. Do not stand near moving equipment.
5. Do not enter trenches without supervisor approval.
6. Do not climb unsafe structures.
7. Do not touch exposed wires.
8. Do not move survey marks without permission.
9. Do not remove pegs or strings carelessly.
10. Keep tools away from walkways.
11. Keep measuring tape away from moving vehicles.
12. Do not joke or fight during practical work.
13. Report injuries immediately.
14. Report unsafe areas.
15. Keep pathways clear.
16. Protect records from damage.
17. Store tools properly.
18. Drink clean water and avoid unsafe water.
19. Obey trainer and supervisor instructions.
20. Respect workers, visitors and community members.
Survey Safety Rules
1. Do not stand in active traffic lanes.
2. Wear reflective jackets when near roads.
3. Do not carry long rods carelessly.
4. Watch for overhead electrical lines.
5. Do not look through instruments without instruction.
6. Do not leave instruments unattended.
7. Protect survey tools from rain, dust and damage.
8. Mark points clearly to prevent tripping.
9. Keep records neat and dry.
10. Report disturbed marks immediately.
Site Management Safety Rules
1. Keep material storage areas organised.
2. Keep waste away from movement paths.
3. Report missing safety equipment.
4. Report workers without protective equipment.

17. Departmental Closing Charge
Survey and Site Management is a sacred and practical field. Through accurate measurement and orderly site management, buildings, roads and community projects can be built safely and properly.
The Survey and Site Management trainee must not look down on careful work. Measurement is important. Records are important. Site order is important. Safety is important. Truthfulness is important.
A site can fail because of wrong measurement. A project can suffer because of poor records. Materials can be wasted because of weak management. Workers can be injured because of poor safety. Therefore, trainees must learn to be accurate, honest, disciplined and responsible.
The Academy is raising trainees who can support builders, engineers, surveyors, supervisors, contractors and communities with faithful service.
