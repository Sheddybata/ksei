Departmental Training Module
Communication Skills
1. Department Overview
The Communication Skills Department trains young people to understand and practise clear, respectful, truthful and effective communication in personal life, workplace settings, ministry, business, leadership, public service and community development.
Communication is the process of sending, receiving, understanding and responding to messages. It includes speaking, listening, writing, reading, body language, tone of voice, public presentation, digital communication, workplace reporting, conflict handling, customer interaction and team communication.
No person can lead well, serve well, work well or build relationships well without communication. Good communication helps people understand instructions, solve problems, build trust, manage conflict, serve customers, present ideas, teach others, lead teams and represent organisations properly.
Poor communication can lead to confusion, quarrels, offence, poor teamwork, wrong instructions, damaged relationships, failed projects and loss of trust. Therefore, trainees must learn to speak wisely, listen carefully, write clearly, ask questions respectfully and respond with self-control.
The Communication Skills Department is not only about speaking English well. It is about communicating with character. A trainee must learn truthfulness, humility, patience, courtesy, confidence, accuracy and wisdom in speech. Words can heal or wound, build or destroy, encourage or discourage. Therefore, communication must be governed by the fear of God.
2. Purpose of the Department
The purpose of the Communication Skills Department is to raise disciplined, confident and God-fearing trainees who can communicate clearly, respectfully and effectively in personal, professional, ministry and community settings.
The department aims to:
1. Teach trainees the meaning and importance of communication.
2. Train trainees in listening, speaking, reading and writing skills.
3. Teach respectful interpersonal communication.
4. Train trainees in workplace communication, reports, meetings and instructions.
5. Build confidence in presentations, group discussions and public interaction.
6. Teach digital communication, email etiquette and responsible messaging.
7. Train trainees to handle conflict, correction and difficult conversations wisely.
8. Develop truthfulness, courtesy, patience, humility and emotional control.
9. Prepare trainees for industrial attachment in offices, businesses, ministries, schools, farms, hospitality centres, workshops, NGOs and public service settings.
10. Help trainees identify enterprise opportunities in communication support services.
11. Develop trainees who can speak with grace, write with clarity and serve with excellence.

8. Safety Rules
Communication may not appear physically dangerous, but words can cause emotional, relational, spiritual, organisational and reputational damage. Trainees must learn communication safety.
General Communication Safety Rules
1. Do not insult people.
2. Do not spread rumours.
3. Do not lie.
4. Do not exaggerate reports.
5. Do not gossip.
6. Do not reveal confidential information.
7. Do not speak harshly to supervisors, colleagues or customers.
8. Do not interrupt rudely.
9. Do not mock people’s accent, grammar or background.
10. Do not use communication to manipulate people.
11. Do not threaten others.
12. Do not use abusive language.
13. Do not forward messages without checking them.
14. Do not share private messages publicly.
15. Do not post harmful content online.
16. Do not argue when correction is given.
17. Do not respond in anger.
18. Do not make promises you cannot keep.
19. Report serious communication conflicts properly.
20. Obey trainer and supervisor instructions at all times.
Digital Communication Safety Rules
1. Check messages before sending.
2. Do not send angry messages.
3. Do not share passwords.
4. Do not post confidential information.
5. Do not spread fake news.
6. Do not bully others online.
7. Do not use indecent language.
8. Do not impersonate others.
9. Do not forward unverified claims.
10. Protect the reputation of the Academy.
Workplace Communication Safety Rules
1. Ask questions when instructions are unclear.
2. Report problems early.
3. Keep official information confidential.
4. Use respectful titles where appropriate.
5. Avoid public embarrassment of colleagues.
6. Do not falsify reports.
7. Use proper channels for complaints.
8. Do not argue with customers.
9. Escalate difficult issues to supervisors.
10. Keep written records where necessary.
Conflict Communication Safety Rules
1. Stay calm.
2. Listen before responding.
3. Avoid shouting.
4. Avoid insults.
5. Avoid threats.
6. Avoid revenge speech.

17. Departmental Closing Charge
Communication Skills is a sacred and practical field. Through communication, people are instructed, comforted, corrected, encouraged, informed, guided and united. Through poor communication, people can be wounded, confused, divided and misled.
A trainee must never treat words carelessly. Words are seeds. Words can build a family, a business, a ministry, a workplace and a nation. Words can also destroy trust, relationships and opportunities.
The Academy is raising trainees who will speak with grace, listen with patience, write with clarity, report with truth, resolve conflict with wisdom and communicate the love and excellence of Christ wherever they serve.
