Departmental Training Module
Project Management
1. Department Overview
The Project Management Department trains young people to understand the basic principles, discipline, tools, records, leadership and practical skills required to plan, organise, implement, monitor and complete projects successfully.
A project is a temporary assignment with a clear beginning, a clear goal, specific activities, required resources, people involved and an expected result. Projects may include building works, road works, farming projects, community service projects, ministry programmes, business start-ups, school events, training activities, health outreaches, youth empowerment programmes and public development initiatives.
Project management helps people turn ideas into organised action. Without project management, good ideas can fail because of poor planning, weak budgeting, unclear responsibilities, poor communication, lack of monitoring, waste, delays and conflict.
This department does not aim to make trainees senior professional project managers within seven months. Rather, it gives them strong foundational knowledge and practical skills in project planning, goal setting, scheduling, budgeting, teamwork, supervision, risk awareness, procurement awareness, monitoring, reporting, evaluation and project closure.
A good project manager must be organised, honest, disciplined, teachable, calm, accountable and able to work with people. Project management requires wisdom, planning, communication, patience, follow-up and responsibility. Trainees must learn that projects do not succeed by desire alone. They succeed through vision, planning, teamwork, diligence and faithful execution.
2. Purpose of the Department
The purpose of the Project Management Department is to raise disciplined, organised and God-fearing trainees who can support projects, lead small assignments and contribute to community development, business growth and organisational effectiveness.
The department aims to:
1. Teach trainees the meaning and importance of project management.
2. Train trainees to turn ideas into clear project goals.
3. Teach project planning, scheduling, budgeting and resource management.
4. Train trainees in teamwork, communication and responsibility allocation.
5. Teach risk identification, problem-solving and safety awareness.
6. Train trainees in monitoring, reporting, documentation and evaluation.
7. Build diligence, accountability, integrity, orderliness and leadership maturity.
8. Prepare trainees for industrial attachment in construction companies, NGOs, ministries, schools, farms, businesses, offices, community organisations and development agencies.
9. Help trainees identify enterprise opportunities in project coordination and administrative support.
10. Develop trainees who can plan, build, serve and lead with Kingdom character.

8. Safety Rules
Project Management involves people, money, time, tools, sites, information and resources. It carries safety, ethical, financial and reputational risks. Trainees must learn to manage projects responsibly.
General Project Safety and Conduct Rules
1. Do not begin any project without approval.
2. Do not spend project money without authorisation.
3. Do not make promises on behalf of the Academy without permission.
4. Do not sign project documents without authority.
5. Do not falsify project reports.
6. Do not hide project problems.
7. Do not ignore safety hazards.
8. Do not assign dangerous tasks to untrained people.
9. Do not misuse project materials.
10. Do not steal project resources.
11. Do not inflate costs dishonestly.
12. Do not favour friends unfairly in project roles.
13. Do not disclose confidential project information.
14. Do not argue with stakeholders.
15. Do not abandon assigned duties.
16. Do not ignore deadlines.
17. Report accidents immediately.
18. Report conflicts early.
19. Keep project records safe.
20. Obey trainer and supervisor instructions at all times.
Financial Safety Rules
1. Keep all receipts.
2. Record every expense.
3. Do not mix personal money with project money.

17. Departmental Closing Charge
Project Management is a sacred and practical field. Through good project management, visions become plans, plans become action, action becomes results and communities are transformed.
A project manager must not be careless with people, money, time, documents or resources. Good project work requires vision, planning, budgeting, scheduling, teamwork, monitoring, reporting, accountability and finishing well.
