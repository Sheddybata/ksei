Dr Isa El-Buba Foundation (DISEF)
Departmental Training Module
Catering Services

1. Module Title
Catering Services

2. Departmental Vision
To raise disciplined, hygienic, skilful, creative and God-fearing trainees who can prepare, present and serve food with excellence, integrity, safety, hospitality and entrepreneurial wisdom.

3. Departmental Mission
The Catering Services Department exists to train young people in practical food preparation, kitchen hygiene, menu planning, food safety, customer service, costing, event catering, baking basics, local and continental dishes, food presentation, business management and industrial attachment readiness.
The department will prepare trainees to become catering assistants, cooks, kitchen supervisors, food vendors, event caterers, restaurant workers, bakery assistants, food service entrepreneurs and community food service leaders.

4. Programme Philosophy
Catering is more than cooking. It is a ministry of service, care, discipline, cleanliness and excellence. Food touches life directly. Poor food handling can cause sickness, loss of trust and business failure. Good food handling can feed families, serve communities, create income, support events and build a good name.
A trainee in Catering Services must understand that taste alone is not enough. Food must be safe, clean, well prepared, properly served, fairly priced and handled with integrity.
The department will therefore train the hands, the mind and the heart. Trainees will learn practical food skills, but they will also learn honesty, cleanliness, punctuality, customer respect, teamwork, humility and stewardship.

5. Key Scripture
1 Corinthians 10:31“Therefore, whether you eat or drink, or whatever you do, do all to the glory of God.”

6. Memory Verse
Proverbs 22:29“Do you see a man who excels in his work? He will stand before kings; he will not stand before unknown men.”

7. Departmental Motto
“Cook with skill, serve with honour, work with cleanliness and build with integrity.”

8. Target Trainees
This module is designed for:
1. Unemployed graduates.
2. Secondary school leavers.
3. Orphans and vulnerable young people.
4. Youths interested in food business.
5. Trainees interested in event catering.
6. Trainees interested in restaurant or hotel work.
7. Trainees interested in baking and snacks production.
8. Trainees interested in self-employment.
9. Trainees who want to learn hospitality and food service.
10. Trainees who may later work in homes, restaurants, schools, hotels, ministries, events, bakeries or food production businesses.

9. Duration
This departmental module runs within the one-year Academy structure.
Phase One: Classroom and Practical Training
Seven months
This phase covers kitchen orientation, food hygiene, basic cooking methods, local dishes, baking basics, menu planning, food costing, event catering, customer service, packaging, record keeping and enterprise preparation.
Phase Two: Industrial Attachment
Five months
This phase places trainees in restaurants, catering businesses, hotels, bakeries, event centres, school kitchens, food production businesses or hospitality organisations for supervised workplace experience.

10. Important Professional Note
This module provides foundational and practical catering training. It does not replace advanced professional training in food science, nutrition, hotel management, dietetics, public health, culinary arts, food technology or regulatory compliance.
Any catering business serving the public must obey relevant food hygiene, business registration, health screening and inspection requirements from appropriate authorities. In Nigeria, food hygiene guidance from NAFDAC and relevant state or local health authorities should be respected. NAFDAC’s food hygiene regulations apply to establishments involved in food preparation, processing, packaging, transportation, distribution, handling, storage and sale.

PART THREE
FOOD SAFETY FOUNDATION
Food safety must be taught from the first week of this department and repeated throughout the year.
1. WHO Five Keys to Safer Food
The World Health Organisation summarises safer food handling through five key messages:
1. Keep clean.
2. Separate raw and cooked food.
3. Cook thoroughly.
4. Keep food at safe temperatures.
5. Use safe water and safe raw materials.
6. These principles are very important for all trainees because catering deals directly with public health.

2. The 4Cs of Food Hygiene
The Food Standards Agency teaches the 4Cs of food hygiene:
1. Cleaning.
2. Cooking.
3. Chilling.
4. Cross-contamination prevention.
5. These principles should become daily habits in the EBOMI kitchen.

3. NAFDAC Food Hygiene Emphasis
NAFDAC’s food hygiene guidance and regulations emphasise hygienic food handling, appropriate training, personal cleanliness, suitable protective clothing, clean premises, safe water supply and proper facilities for cleaning and sanitation in food establishments.
Trainees must therefore understand that food hygiene is not optional. It is a responsibility before God, customers, the community and the law.

UNIT 3
Personal Hygiene for Food Handlers
Purpose
To teach trainees personal cleanliness and responsible conduct when handling food.
Learning Objectives
By the end of this unit, trainees should be able to:
1. Explain personal hygiene.
2. Demonstrate proper handwashing.
3. Dress appropriately for kitchen work.
4. Understand when not to handle food.
5. Avoid habits that contaminate food.
6. Maintain cleanliness throughout food preparation.
Teaching Content
Personal hygiene means keeping the body, clothing and habits clean so that food is not contaminated.
A food handler should:
1. Wash hands before food preparation.
2. Wash hands after using the toilet.
3. Wash hands after coughing, sneezing or touching the face.
4. Keep nails short and clean.
5. Wear clean apron.
6. Cover hair with hairnet or scarf.
7. Avoid jewellery during food preparation.
8. Cover wounds properly.
9. Avoid handling food when sick.
10. Avoid tasting food with the cooking spoon and returning it to the pot.
11. Avoid touching cooked food with dirty hands.
12. Avoid chewing gum or eating while preparing food.
13. Avoid coughing over food.
14. Wear suitable protective clothing where required.
15. NAFDAC food hygiene regulation materials emphasise that food handlers should maintain high personal cleanliness, wear suitable clean protective clothing and be free from diseases or open lesions likely to contaminate food.
Practical Demonstration
Demonstrate proper handwashing and correct dressing for kitchen work.
Group Activity
Ask trainees to identify bad personal hygiene habits in a role-play kitchen scene.
Assessment
Trainees should demonstrate proper handwashing and list ten personal hygiene rules.
Character Focus
Cleanliness
Trainees should understand that cleanliness is part of responsibility and care for others.

UNIT 4
Cleaning, Cooking, Chilling and Avoiding Cross-Contamination
Purpose
To teach trainees the core food hygiene principles that protect customers from foodborne illness.
Learning Objectives
By the end of this unit, trainees should be able to:
1. Explain cleaning.
2. Explain proper cooking.
3. Explain chilling and safe storage.
4. Explain cross-contamination.
5. Separate raw and cooked foods.
6. Maintain safe kitchen practices.
Teaching Content
Food safety requires constant attention. The 4Cs are:
1. Cleaning.
2. Cooking.
3. Chilling.
4. Cross-contamination prevention.
A. Cleaning
Cleaning includes hands, surfaces, utensils, equipment, floors and storage areas.
B. Cooking
Food must be cooked thoroughly, especially meat, poultry, eggs, fish and seafood. WHO’s safer food guidance includes cooking thoroughly as one of the five essential keys to safer food.
C. Chilling
Food that needs cold storage should not be left carelessly in heat. Refrigeration, freezing and proper cooling help reduce spoilage and unsafe handling.
D. Cross-Contamination
Cross-contamination happens when harmful germs are transferred from one food, surface, hand or tool to another. Raw meat, poultry, fish and eggs must be handled carefully and separated from ready-to-eat food.
Practical Demonstration
Demonstrate:
1. Separating raw and cooked foods.
2. Using separate chopping boards where available.
3. Cleaning surfaces before and after preparation.
4. Covering cooked food.
5. Storing food properly.
6. Washing utensils after raw food contact.
Group Activity
Set up a mock kitchen with hygiene errors. Ask trainees to identify and correct them.
Assessment
Trainees should explain the 4Cs and give one example of each.
Character Focus
Responsibility
Trainees should protect others by preventing contamination.
