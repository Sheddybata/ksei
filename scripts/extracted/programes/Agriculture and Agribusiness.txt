Departmental Training Module
Agriculture and Agribusiness

1. Department Overview
The Agriculture and Agribusiness Department trains young people to understand farming as a calling, a skill, a business and a tool for community development.
The department teaches trainees how to produce food, care for the soil, plant crops, manage simple farm systems, keep farm records, reduce waste, market farm products and build small agricultural enterprises.
Agriculture is not only about planting crops. It includes planning, soil care, seed selection, nursery work, irrigation, composting, pest control, harvesting, storage, processing, marketing, record keeping and business management.
Agribusiness means treating agriculture as a productive enterprise. It teaches trainees to think beyond subsistence farming and begin to understand farming as a source of income, employment, food security and national development.
This department is important because many communities need food, jobs, productive youth and sustainable rural development. A well-trained agriculture trainee can become a farmer, farm assistant, agribusiness operator, cooperative member, food processor, farm record keeper, input supplier or community food security worker.

2. Purpose of the Department
The purpose of the Agriculture and Agribusiness Department is to raise disciplined, skilled and God-fearing trainees who can use agriculture to feed families, create income, serve communities and contribute to national development.
The department aims to:
1. Teach trainees the dignity of farming and food production.
2. Build practical knowledge of crop and basic livestock production.
3. Train trainees in soil preparation, planting, farm maintenance and harvesting.
4. Teach simple farm planning, budgeting and record keeping.
5. Help trainees understand agribusiness opportunities.
6. Build discipline, patience, diligence and stewardship through farm work.
7. Prepare trainees for industrial attachment in farms, agribusinesses, agricultural offices, food production centres and related organisations.
8. Equip trainees to start small agricultural enterprises after graduation.

5. Weekly Topics for Seven Months
The seven-month classroom and practical phase should be divided into twenty-eight training weeks. Each week should include teaching, demonstration, supervised practical work, group discussion, farm record keeping and character formation.

Month 1: Foundation of Agriculture, Discipline and Farm Orientation
Week 1: Introduction to Agriculture and Food Security
Teaching Focus
Meaning of agriculture, importance of food production, agriculture as service to God and society, dignity of farm work.
Scripture for the Week
Genesis 2:15“And the LORD God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it.”
Daily Practical Activities
1. Day 1: Walk around the farm area and identify available land, water sources and access points.
2. Day 2: List common crops grown in the community and discuss their uses.
3. Day 3: Identify farm problems in the community, such as poor soil, pests, lack of water or poor storage.
4. Day 4: Clean a small farm area as a group.
5. Day 5: Write a simple reflection on why food production is important.
Character Lesson
Agriculture begins with stewardship. God expects people to care for the land, not waste it.

2. Day 2: Demonstrate safe carrying and storage of cutlasses, hoes, rakes and shovels.
3. Day 3: Practise cleaning tools after use.

5. Day 5: Complete a farm safety checklist.

Orderliness prevents accidents and helps work move smoothly.

8. Safety Rules
Safety must be taught repeatedly. Agriculture can be dangerous when tools, chemicals, animals, water, fire or machinery are handled carelessly.
General Farm Safety Rules
1. Do not use any tool without instruction.
2. Carry cutlasses, hoes and sharp tools safely.
3. Do not run while holding farm tools.
4. Wear boots or strong shoes on the farm.
5. Wear gloves where necessary.
6. Do not joke or fight during practical work.
7. Keep tools away from pathways.
8. Return tools after use.
9. Do not apply chemicals without trainer approval.
10. Wash hands after farm work.
11. Do not drink unsafe farm water.
12. Report injuries immediately.
13. Report snake holes, sharp objects and unsafe areas.
14. Do not work alone in risky areas.
15. Keep the farm clean.
16. Avoid working during dangerous weather.
17. Use protective clothing during input handling.
18. Keep children and visitors away from tools.
19. Follow trainer instructions at all times.
20. Do not eat unwashed produce from the farm.
Safety Around Animals
1. Do not enter animal housing without permission.
2. Wash hands after touching animals.
3. Do not handle sick animals without supervision.
4. Keep animal houses clean.
5. Report dead or sick animals immediately.
6. Do not feed animals unknown materials.
7. Do not tease animals.
8. Keep feed and water containers clean.
Safety Around Farm Inputs
1. Store inputs safely.
2. Read labels where available.
3. Do not smell or taste farm chemicals.
4. Use gloves and masks when instructed.
5. Wash hands after handling inputs.
6. Do not mix inputs without instruction.
7. Keep inputs away from food and drinking water.
8. Report spills immediately.
Safety Scripture
Proverbs 22:3“A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself: but the simple pass on, and are punished.”
Safety Character Lesson
Safety is wisdom. A careless trainee can harm himself, herself, others, animals, crops and the reputation of the Academy.

2. Show the tool or material needed.
3. State safety rules.
4. Demonstrate slowly.
5. Explain each step.
6. Let one trainee repeat under supervision.
7. Correct mistakes gently.
8. Allow group practice.
9. Review quality.
10. Record lesson in farm logbook.
Demonstration Scripture
Proverbs 4:7“Wisdom is the principal thing; therefore get wisdom: and with all thy getting get understanding.”
Demonstration Character Lesson
Trainees should not only copy actions. They must understand why each step matters.

12. Character Lessons Linked to the Department
Agriculture is one of the best departments for character training because farming teaches patience, diligence and stewardship.

A. Diligence
Crops do not grow well when farmers are lazy. Trainees must water, weed, observe and maintain the farm regularly.
Scripture
Proverbs 12:11“He that tilleth his land shall be satisfied with bread: but he that followeth vain persons is void of understanding.”
Character Lesson
Hard work produces fruit. Laziness produces lack.

B. Patience
Seeds do not become harvest overnight. Trainees must learn to wait, nurture and trust the process.
Scripture
James 5:7“Be patient therefore, brethren, unto the coming of the Lord. Behold, the husbandman waiteth for the precious fruit of the earth.”
Character Lesson
Growth takes time. A trainee must not give up because results are not immediate.

C. Stewardship
Land, seeds, tools, water and produce must be managed carefully.
Scripture
Genesis 2:15“And the LORD God took the man, and put him into the garden of Eden to dress it and to keep it.”
Character Lesson
God expects people to care for what He gives them.

D. Faithfulness in Small Things
Small daily tasks affect the final harvest.
Scripture
Luke 16:10“He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much.”
Character Lesson
Little duties matter. Faithfulness in small tasks prepares trainees for greater responsibility.

E. Teamwork
Farm work often requires group effort.
Scripture
Ecclesiastes 4:9“Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour.”
Character Lesson
Working together increases productivity and strengthens unity.

F. Responsibility
A plant can die if neglected. Animals can suffer if not cared for.
Scripture
Proverbs 27:23“Be thou diligent to know the state of thy flocks, and look well to thy herds.”
Character Lesson
Responsible people observe what is entrusted to them.

G. Integrity
Farm records, market prices, measurements and sales must be truthful.
Scripture
Proverbs 11:1“A false balance is abomination to the LORD: but a just weight is his delight.”
Character Lesson
Dishonesty destroys trust. A Kingdom farmer or agribusiness person must be truthful.

H. Humility
No honest work is shameful. Farming is honourable.
Scripture
Proverbs 14:23“In all labour there is profit: but the talk of the lips tendeth only to penury.”
Character Lesson
Productive labour has dignity. Trainees must not despise farm work.
