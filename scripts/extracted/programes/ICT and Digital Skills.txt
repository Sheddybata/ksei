Dr Isa El-Buba Foundation (DISEF)
Departmental Training Module
ICT and Digital Skills
1. Module Title
ICT and Digital Skills
2. Departmental Vision
To raise disciplined, digitally competent, creative, ethical and God-fearing trainees who can use technology responsibly for learning, work, business, communication, service, productivity and community transformation.
3. Departmental Mission
The ICT and Digital Skills Department exists to train young people in practical computer use, digital literacy, office productivity, internet research, online communication, cybersecurity awareness, digital content creation, basic data handling, artificial intelligence awareness, digital entrepreneurship and workplace readiness.
The department will prepare trainees to become office assistants, digital clerks, ICT support assistants, content creators, data entry assistants, online business operators, digital entrepreneurs, administrative workers, social media assistants and responsible users of technology.
4. Programme Philosophy
Technology is a tool. It can be used to build or destroy, to serve or deceive, to learn or waste time, to create opportunities or spread confusion. EBOMI Kingdom Leadership Academy must therefore train trainees not only to use technology, but to use it wisely, responsibly and ethically.
ICT and digital skills are now important in education, business, government, ministry, agriculture, construction, health, administration, communication and entrepreneurship. A trainee who cannot use basic digital tools may struggle in the modern workplace. A trainee who uses digital tools without character may become a danger to society.
Therefore, this department trains both competence and conscience. Trainees must learn computer skills, but they must also learn truthfulness, online discipline, digital safety, respect, privacy, productivity and Kingdom responsibility.

8. Target Trainees
This module is designed for:
1. Unemployed graduates.
2. Secondary school leavers.
3. Orphans and vulnerable young people.
4. Youths with little or no computer experience.
5. Trainees preparing for office, business or administrative work.
6. Trainees interested in digital entrepreneurship.
7. Trainees interested in content creation and online communication.
8. Trainees interested in data entry, digital records and ICT support.
9. Trainees in other departments who need digital skills for their trade.
10. Trainees preparing for industrial attachment in offices, businesses, schools, ministries, farms, construction companies or ICT centres.
9. Duration
This departmental module runs within the one-year Academy structure.
Phase One: Classroom and Practical Training
Seven months
This phase covers computer fundamentals, typing, file management, office productivity, internet use, email, digital communication, presentations, spreadsheets, data handling, content creation, cybersecurity, responsible AI use and digital enterprise.
Phase Two: Industrial Attachment
Five months
This phase places trainees in offices, ICT centres, schools, business centres, administrative departments, digital media units, farms, companies, ministries, NGOs or enterprises where they can practise digital skills in real workplace settings.
10. Important Professional Note
This module provides foundational and practical ICT and digital skills training. It does not replace advanced professional training in computer science, software engineering, cybersecurity, data science, networking, digital forensics, artificial intelligence engineering or information systems management.
Trainees must be taught to recognise their level. They should not claim advanced technical competence beyond what they have learnt. They must also respect data privacy, organisational rules, copyright, confidentiality and lawful technology use.
PART ONE
DIGITAL COMPETENCE FOUNDATION
This department should be guided by recognised digital competence principles.
The European DigComp framework describes digital competence as the confident, critical and responsible use of digital technologies for learning, work and participation in society. It highlights areas such as information and data literacy, communication and collaboration, digital content creation, safety and problem-solving.
UNESCO’s ICT Competency Framework for Teachers identifies ICT competencies needed for education, curriculum support, assessment, pedagogy, organisation, administration and professional development.
Nigeria’s National Digital Literacy Framework provides guidelines for developing digital literacy in Nigeria, and NITDA’s digital literacy work is part of national efforts to increase participation in the digital economy.
UNESCO’s AI Competency Framework for Students encourages education systems to prepare learners to engage with artificial intelligence safely, meaningfully and responsibly.
This EBOMI module therefore focuses on practical digital ability, workplace usefulness, ethical conduct, cybersecurity awareness and Kingdom character.

UNIT 10
Cybersecurity, Password Safety and Online Protection
Purpose
To teach trainees how to protect themselves, their devices and organisational information online.
Learning Objectives
By the end of this unit, trainees should be able to:
1. Explain cybersecurity in simple terms.
2. Create strong passwords.
3. Understand multi-factor authentication.
4. Recognise phishing.
5. Avoid suspicious links.
6. Update software where possible.
7. Protect personal information.
8. Report online threats.
Teaching Content
Cybersecurity means protecting computers, phones, accounts, networks and information from theft, damage, fraud or misuse.
CISA identifies basic cybersecurity practices such as using strong passwords, updating software, thinking before clicking suspicious links and turning on multi-factor authentication.
Strong Password Habits
Trainees should:
1. Use long passwords.
2. Avoid obvious passwords.
3. Avoid using only names or birthdays.
4. Avoid sharing passwords.
5. Avoid using the same password everywhere.
6. Change passwords when compromised.
7. Use password managers where appropriate and understood.
8. Enable multi-factor authentication where available.
9. CISA explains that multi-factor authentication adds another method of verifying identity and helps prevent unauthorised access to accounts and applications.
Phishing
Phishing is when criminals try to trick people into revealing passwords, bank details or personal information.
Warning signs include:
1. Urgent threats.
2. Unknown sender.
3. Suspicious link.
4. Poor spelling.
5. Requests for password.
6. Unexpected attachment.
7. Fake prize.
8. Bank verification request from unknown source.
9. Strange email address.
10. Pressure to act quickly.
Practical Demonstration
Show trainees examples of:
1. Strong and weak passwords.
2. Suspicious email.
3. Safe and unsafe links.
4. Fake login page.
5. Secure website signs.
6. Account recovery options.
Group Activity
Trainees should identify phishing warning signs from sample messages.
Assessment
Trainees should list five cybersecurity habits and identify phishing examples.
Character Focus
