Departmental Training Module
Hospitality Management

1. Department Overview
The Hospitality Management Department trains young people to understand the principles, discipline, character, skills and practical service required in the hospitality industry.
Hospitality is the act of receiving, serving, caring for and creating comfort for guests, clients, visitors and customers. It includes hotel service, guest house management, front desk service, housekeeping, food and beverage service, event support, customer relations, facility care, bookings, guest records and service excellence.
Hospitality is not only about hotels. It is useful in ministries, conference centres, restaurants, schools, hospitals, guest houses, offices, retreats, events, tourism centres, catering businesses and community service projects.
This department teaches trainees to serve with excellence, humility, cleanliness, courtesy, emotional control, punctuality and integrity. A hospitality worker must be polite, neat, alert, respectful, patient and trustworthy.
A careless hospitality worker can offend guests, damage the reputation of an organisation, spread infection through poor hygiene, mismanage bookings, lose property or create disorder. Therefore, trainees must learn that hospitality is a ministry of care, order and honour.

2. Purpose of the Department
The purpose of the Hospitality Management Department is to raise disciplined, courteous and God-fearing trainees who can serve guests, manage basic hospitality duties, support events and work in hospitality-related organisations with excellence.
The department aims to:
1. Teach trainees the meaning and value of hospitality.
2. Build a culture of honour, service and guest care.
3. Train trainees in front desk, reception and customer service basics.
4. Teach housekeeping, cleaning, room preparation and facility care.
5. Train trainees in food and beverage service awareness.
6. Teach event support, table setting, booking records and guest handling.
7. Build discipline, patience, courtesy, cleanliness and emotional maturity.
8. Prepare trainees for industrial attachment in hotels, guest houses, event centres, restaurants, conference centres, ministries, hospitals and service organisations.
9. Help trainees identify enterprise opportunities in hospitality.
10. Develop trainees who can serve people with excellence, humility and Kingdom character.

8. Safety Rules
Hospitality work includes physical, hygiene, food safety, guest safety, property and ethical risks. Trainees must learn to protect guests, themselves, the organisation and the reputation of the Academy.
General Hospitality Safety Rules
1. Do not enter guest rooms without permission.
2. Do not touch guest property unnecessarily.
3. Do not keep lost property privately.
4. Do not disclose guest information.
5. Do not argue with guests.
6. Do not serve food with dirty hands.
7. Do not use cleaning chemicals without instruction.
8. Do not mix chemicals carelessly.
9. Do not leave wet floors without warning signs.
10. Do not block walkways.
11. Do not carry hot food carelessly.
12. Do not run while serving.
13. Do not lift heavy items alone when help is needed.
14. Do not ignore broken furniture or electrical faults.
15. Do not leave sharp objects carelessly.
16. Do not eat from guest food.
17. Do not gossip about guests.
18. Report accidents immediately.
19. Report lost items immediately.
20. Obey trainer and supervisor instructions at all times.
Housekeeping Safety Rules
1. Wear gloves when cleaning toilets and bathrooms.
2. Wash hands after cleaning.
3. Keep cleaning chemicals labelled.
4. Store chemicals safely.
5. Keep cleaning tools clean.
6. Report broken glass immediately.
7. Use wet floor warning signs.
8. Do not climb unsafe furniture.
9. Do not overload laundry baskets.
10. Report damaged room items.
Food Service Safety Rules
1. Wash hands before food service.
2. Keep food covered.
3. Do not serve food that looks or smells unsafe.
4. Do not touch ready-to-eat food with bare hands.
5. Use clean plates and cutlery.
6. Do not cough over food.
7. Keep hot food and drinks away from children’s reach.
8. Carry trays carefully.
9. Report food spills immediately.
10. Respect dietary instructions.
Guest Safety and Confidentiality Rules
1. Protect guest privacy.
2. Do not announce guest room numbers carelessly.
3. Do not share guest records.
4. Do not open rooms for unknown persons.
5. Report suspicious behaviour.
6. Protect children, elderly guests and vulnerable guests.
7. Escalate complaints to supervisors.
8. Keep emergency exits clear.
9. Report fire hazards.
10. Keep guest areas clean and safe.
Safety Scripture
Proverbs 22:3“A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself: but the simple pass on, and are punished.”
Safety Character Lesson
Safety is wisdom. A disciplined hospitality trainee protects guests, property, health, records and the reputation of the Academy.

17. Departmental Closing Charge
Hospitality Management is a sacred and practical field. Through hospitality, strangers are welcomed, guests are comforted, families are served, events are supported, organisations are strengthened and communities experience care.

The Academy is raising trainees who will not look down on service work. They must become men and women who can receive people well, serve with joy, protect guest property, keep clean environments and honour God through excellence.
